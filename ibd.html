Choosing the first drug

Two societies, two philosophies. ACG 2025 declines to rank the advanced therapies in Crohn's; AGA publishes efficacy tiers from network meta-analysis. Knowing which one you are quoting matters more than knowing either.

Do not make them fail conventional therapy first

The headline change of ACG 2025

"We suggest against requiring failure of conventional therapy before initiation of advanced therapy for the management of Crohn's disease." Early advanced therapy beats accelerated step-up. The old reflex of exhausting steroids and thiopurines before reaching for a biologic is now explicitly discouraged.

CD Rec 3 · conditional, low

ACG declines to rank the advanced therapies in Crohn's

No recommendation favours one agent over another

In advanced-therapy-naive Crohn's, ACG 2025 deliberately makes no comparative recommendation. AGA does rank them. If you want a league table you are using AGA, not ACG — and you should know which one you are quoting.

CDsocieties differ

The one comparative statement in Crohn's

Risankizumab over ustekinumab, after anti-TNF

The single head-to-head-derived positioning statement in the Crohn's guideline, translating SEQUENCE directly. Applies to anti-TNF-experienced patients only.

CD Rec 19 · conditional, low

Use your best agent first — in UC this is stated outright

Response rates fall after each failure

"Patients with moderately to severely active UC have higher rates of response and remission with their first therapies than after failure of one or more other advanced therapies." The corollary: sequencing weak-to-strong wastes the best chance you will get.

UC key concept

Infliximab is the preferred anti-TNF in UC

Stated explicitly

And vedolizumab is recommended over adalimumab for induction and maintenance, reflecting VARSITY. Where infection risk is high, vedolizumab or an anti-IL-23 strategy may be preferred over more systemically immunosuppressive options.

UC Rec 45 · strong, moderate

ACG will not build an efficacy league table from network meta-analyses

And says so

"Post hoc subgroup analyses and network meta-analyses provide hypothesis-generating data but are not sufficient to stratify therapies for individual patients." AGA takes the opposite methodological view and publishes tiers. Both positions are defensible; know which you are relying on.

The head-to-head trials

Only three exist in Crohn's and one in UC. Everything else is placebo-controlled and compared indirectly.

SEQUENCE

Risankizumab vs ustekinumab · Crohn's

n=520, all with prior anti-TNF failure. Clinical remission at week 24 58.6% vs 39.5% — non-inferiority met. Endoscopic remission at week 48 31.8% vs 16.2%, superior, p<0.001. Serious adverse events 10.3% vs 17.4%.

Risankizumab wins on endoscopy. This is the trial behind ACG Rec 19.

GALAXI 2 and 3

Guselkumab vs ustekinumab · Crohn's

n=1,021, treat-through, triple-dummy. Pooled 48 weeks, guselkumab 200 mg q4w vs ustekinumab: endoscopic response 52.7% vs 37.1% (p<0.001), endoscopic remission 37.2% vs 24.7% (p=0.001), deep remission 33.8% vs 22.3% (p=0.002). Clinical remission alone 70.3% vs 62.9% — not significant (p=0.058).

Guselkumab wins on every endoscopic endpoint but not on symptoms alone. A clean illustration of why the endpoint you choose decides the answer.

VIVID-1

Mirikizumab vs ustekinumab · Crohn's

Treat-through, randomised 6:3:2. Versus placebo at week 52: endoscopic response 38.0% vs 9.0%, CDAI remission 45.4% vs 19.6%. Versus ustekinumab: non-inferiority demonstrated for CDAI remission at week 52; in biologic-exposed patients numerically higher but not significant.

A tie, not a win. Mirikizumab is non-inferior to ustekinumab — do not read it as superior.

SEAVUE

Ustekinumab vs adalimumab · Crohn's

Biologic-naive, 56 weeks, double-dummy. Week 52 clinical remission 65% vs 61% and endoscopic remission 29% vs 31% — no significant difference on either. Ustekinumab had numerically fewer infections and longer persistence.

A genuine draw. Indirect comparison had predicted adalimumab superiority; SEAVUE refuted it. A standing warning about network meta-analyses.

VARSITY

Vedolizumab vs adalimumab · UC

n=769, 52 weeks, double-dummy. Clinical remission 31.3% vs 22.5% (p=0.006); endoscopic improvement 39.7% vs 27.7% (p<0.001). But corticosteroid-free remission 12.6% vs 21.8%, numerically favouring adalimumab. Infections 23.4 vs 34.6 per 100 patient-years.

Vedolizumab wins the primary endpoint and loses the steroid-free one. Critical caveat: the double-dummy design forbade dose optimisation of either drug, which limits real-world transfer.
LUCENT, ELEVATE, QUASAR and ARTEMIS are not head-to-head trials — all are placebo-controlled. As of August 2026 VARSITY remains the only completed head-to-head biologic trial in ulcerative colitis, which is why UC positioning leans on network meta-analysis in a way Crohn's positioning need not.

What the guidelines recommend against

The negative recommendations are the ones that change behaviour, because they name things people still do.

Oral mesalamine in Crohn's disease

Induction or maintenance in mild-to-moderate CD

The carve-out: sulfasalazine 3–6 g/day may be modestly effective in mild colonic or ileocolonic Crohn's — but not in isolated small bowel disease.

CD Rec 8 · STRONG, moderate

Ileal-release budesonide to maintain remission

Not targets

Histological remission is not a treatment target in Crohn's disease. Transmural healing is not a target either — it is an adjunct to endoscopic remission. In UC, ACG 2025 says histologic remission "has not yet been validated prospectively as a preferred target." The VERDICT trial is the study designed to settle whether chasing histology is worth it.

Where the two ACG guidelines diverge

The ACG UC guideline endorses STRIDE-II and sets endoscopic improvement as a graded recommendation (Rec 4, strong). The ACG Crohn's guideline never mentions STRIDE or treat-to-target at all — it states mucosal healing is "a goal of therapy" and that objective evaluation should be done "periodically", with no interval and no numeric biomarker target. That asymmetry is real, not an oversight in this page.

The assessment window

"Clinical evidence of improvement should be evident within 2–4 weeks, and the maximal improvement should occur by 12–16 weeks." Judge a drug at 12–16 weeks, not at 4.

Why symptoms are not enough

"Symptoms of CD do not correlate well with the presence of active inflammation." And conversely, biomarkers "should not exclusively serve as a treatment endpoint because normalization of the biomarker may occur in the presence of active mucosal inflammation/ulceration." Neither symptoms nor calprotectin alone will do.

The evidence it rests on

CALM: tight control targeting biomarkers plus symptoms achieved 46% endoscopic healing at 48 weeks, an adjusted risk difference of +16.1% over symptom-driven management (p=0.01), with fewer hospitalisations — 13.2 vs 28.0 per 100 patient-years.

After Crohn's surgery

Roughly half of patients have symptomatic recurrence within five years, and endoscopic recurrence comes first — which is the whole reason for scoping at 6 to 12 months rather than waiting for symptoms.

Scope them at 6 to 12 months

Ileocolonoscopy after ileocolic resection

"In patients with surgically induced remission of CD, we suggest postoperative endoscopic assessment at 6–12 months over no monitoring." Every current source agrees on this window — ACG, AGA and the Crohn's and Colitis Canada pathway. Later assessment correlates with worse outcomes.

Do not reach for these

Non-invasive monitoring between scopes

Calprotectin and ultrasound

Faecal calprotectin at a cut-off of 100–150 µg/g gives 70–89% sensitivity, 58–69% specificity and a negative predictive value above 90% for endoscopic recurrence. Intestinal ultrasound is emerging as a radiation-free alternative in expert hands. A raised calprotectin should bring the colonoscopy forward.

Drain before you immunosuppress

"We suggest that an intra-abdominal abscess (>2 cm) be treated with antibiotics and a drainage procedure, and immunosuppression held until drainage is achieved." Radiologically or surgically.

No response or loss of response

The decisive question is never "is the patient better?" but "is there active inflammation, and is there enough drug on board to explain it?"

Step by step

1

Confirm active inflammation

Symptoms alone are unreliable. Objectively re-stage before touching the dose.

  • CRP, faecal calprotectin
  • Endoscopy ± cross-sectional imaging (MRE / intestinal ultrasound)
  • Exclude C. difficile and CMV — especially UC on steroids
2

Exclude non-inflammatory mimics

Normal calprotectin with ongoing symptoms redirects the workup entirely.

  • IBS-type overlap, visceral hypersensitivity
  • Bile-acid diarrhoea, SIBO
  • Fibrostenotic stricture, abscess, fistula, short bowel
  • Adherence and injection technique
3

Therapeutic drug monitoring

Reactive TDM at the point of failure separates pharmacokinetic failure — fixable by dose — from mechanistic failure, fixable only by switching class.

  • Drug trough level plus anti-drug antibodies
  • Best validated for anti-TNF agents
4

Act on the pattern

Three results, three different moves. Also treat the steroid problem: any patient needing a second steroid course within 12 months, or unable to taper below 10 mg prednisone, has failed their maintenance strategy.

The three patterns

Low drug · no antibodies

Pharmacokinetic failure → intensify

Increase the dose, shorten the interval, or both. Consider adding an immunomodulator to raise exposure with anti-TNF. Re-check the level after the change rather than assuming it worked.

Low drug · high anti-drug antibodies

Immunogenic failure → switch

Low-titre transient antibodies may be overcome by dose escalation plus an immunomodulator. High-titre persistent antibodies mean the drug is gone: switch within class — antibodies are drug-specific, not class-wide.

Adequate drug · active inflammation

Mechanistic failure → change class

More of the same drug will not help. After anti-TNF primary non-response, out-of-class switching outperforms within-class; after secondary loss of response with immunogenicity, within-class is reasonable.

Acute severe ulcerative colitis

A hospital emergency with a defined decision timetable. Truelove & Witts: ≥ 6 bloody stools/day plus any of temperature > 37.8 °C, HR > 90, Hb < 10.5 g/dL, ESR > 30 mm/h.

Day 0–1 · Admit
  • IV methylprednisolone 60 mg/day, or hydrocortisone 100 mg 6–8-hourly. Higher doses add nothing.
  • Flexible sigmoidoscopy within 24 h — biopsy for CMV; stool for C. difficile.
  • Prophylactic LMWH — VTE risk is high despite rectal bleeding.
  • Stop opioids, anticholinergics, NSAIDs, antidiarrhoeals. Early surgical co-review.
  • Abdominal X-ray: exclude toxic megacolon.
Day 3 · Predict failure
  • Oxford index: > 8 stools/day, or 3–8 stools/day with CRP > 45 mg/L → roughly 85% risk of colectomy on steroids alone.
  • This is the moment to commit to rescue therapy — not day 7.
  • Confirm the surgical plan in parallel; rescue is not an alternative to a surgeon.
Day 3–5 · Rescue
  • ACG 2025 makes this a strong recommendation: "In patients with ASUC failing to adequately respond to IV corticosteroids by 3 days, we recommend medical rescue therapy with infliximab or cyclosporine." UC Rec 51 · STRONG, moderate
  • Infliximab 5 mg/kg IV. Accelerated dosing — 2nd and 3rd doses within days rather than weeks 2 and 6 — is used with high inflammatory burden or albumin < 30 g/L. Retrospective data only.
  • Ciclosporin 2 mg/kg/day IV continuous infusion, target trough 150–250 ng/mL, then oral 4–8 mg/kg/day. Equivalent to infliximab in CYSIF and CONSTRUCT. Avoid if cholesterol < 3 mmol/L or magnesium is low.
  • JAK inhibitors are not endorsed here. ACG 2025 is explicit: in ASUC failing IV steroids by day 3, or failing infliximab induction, "there are insufficient data to routinely recommend treatment with tofacitinib or upadacitinib." Cohort data are positive but this remains off-label and outside the guideline. UC
  • Sequential rescue carries high infection risk — specialist centres only.
After a successful rescue
  • Infliximab responders → maintain on infliximab. UC Rec 52 · strong, moderate
  • Ciclosporin responders → bridge to a thiopurine UC Rec 53 · conditional, low or to vedolizumab UC Rec 54 · conditional, very low
  • Ciclosporin is a bridge, never a maintenance drug. Decide the destination before you start the infusion.
Day 5–7 · Decide
  • No clear response to rescue by day 5–7 → colectomy. Delay past this point increases postoperative morbidity and mortality.
  • Colectomy is a treatment, not a failure — frame it that way with the patient early.
  • If rescued, plan maintenance immediately: infliximab maintenance, or thiopurine bridging after ciclosporin.
