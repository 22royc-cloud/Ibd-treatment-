No response or loss of response

The decisive question is never "is the patient better?" but "is there active inflammation, and is there enough drug on board to explain it?"

Step by step

1

Confirm active inflammation

Symptoms alone are unreliable. Objectively re-stage before touching the dose.

  • CRP, faecal calprotectin
  • Endoscopy ± cross-sectional imaging (MRE / intestinal ultrasound)
  • Exclude C. difficile and CMV — especially UC on steroids
2

Exclude non-inflammatory mimics

Normal calprotectin with ongoing symptoms redirects the workup entirely.

  • IBS-type overlap, visceral hypersensitivity
  • Bile-acid diarrhoea, SIBO
  • Fibrostenotic stricture, abscess, fistula, short bowel
  • Adherence and injection technique
3

Therapeutic drug monitoring

Reactive TDM at the point of failure separates pharmacokinetic failure — fixable by dose — from mechanistic failure, fixable only by switching class.

  • Drug trough level plus anti-drug antibodies
  • Best validated for anti-TNF agents
4

Act on the pattern

Three results, three different moves. Also treat the steroid problem: any patient needing a second steroid course within 12 months, or unable to taper below 10 mg prednisone, has failed their maintenance strategy.

The three patterns

Low drug · no antibodies

Pharmacokinetic failure → intensify

Increase the dose, shorten the interval, or both. Consider adding an immunomodulator to raise exposure with anti-TNF. Re-check the level after the change rather than assuming it worked.

Low drug · high anti-drug antibodies

Immunogenic failure → switch

Low-titre transient antibodies may be overcome by dose escalation plus an immunomodulator. High-titre persistent antibodies mean the drug is gone: switch within class — antibodies are drug-specific, not class-wide.

Adequate drug · active inflammation

Mechanistic failure → change class

More of the same drug will not help. After anti-TNF primary non-response, out-of-class switching outperforms within-class; after secondary loss of response with immunogenicity, within-class is reasonable.

Acute severe ulcerative colitis

A hospital emergency with a defined decision timetable. Truelove & Witts: ≥ 6 bloody stools/day plus any of temperature > 37.8 °C, HR > 90, Hb < 10.5 g/dL, ESR > 30 mm/h.

Day 0–1 · Admit
  • IV methylprednisolone 60 mg/day, or hydrocortisone 100 mg 6–8-hourly. Higher doses add nothing.
  • Flexible sigmoidoscopy within 24 h — biopsy for CMV; stool for C. difficile.
  • Prophylactic LMWH — VTE risk is high despite rectal bleeding.
  • Stop opioids, anticholinergics, NSAIDs, antidiarrhoeals. Early surgical co-review.
  • Abdominal X-ray: exclude toxic megacolon.
Day 3 · Predict failure
  • Oxford index: > 8 stools/day, or 3–8 stools/day with CRP > 45 mg/L → roughly 85% risk of colectomy on steroids alone.
  • This is the moment to commit to rescue therapy — not day 7.
  • Confirm the surgical plan in parallel; rescue is not an alternative to a surgeon.
Day 3–5 · Rescue
  • Infliximab 5 mg/kg IV. Accelerated dosing — 2nd and 3rd doses within days rather than weeks 2 and 6 — is used with high inflammatory burden or albumin < 30 g/L. Retrospective data only.
  • Ciclosporin 2 mg/kg/day IV continuous infusion, target trough 150–250 ng/mL, then oral 4–8 mg/kg/day bridging to a thiopurine. Equivalent to infliximab in CYSIF and CONSTRUCT. Avoid if cholesterol < 3 mmol/L or magnesium is low.
  • Tofacitinib 10 mg three times daily and upadacitinib 45 mg have positive cohort data as rescue — off-label
  • Sequential rescue carries high infection risk — specialist centres only.
Day 5–7 · Decide
  • No clear response to rescue by day 5–7 → colectomy. Delay past this point increases postoperative morbidity and mortality.
  • Colectomy is a treatment, not a failure — frame it that way with the patient early.
  • If rescued, plan maintenance immediately: infliximab maintenance, or thiopurine bridging after ciclosporin.

Pipeline

Not yet approved as of August 2026. Positive phase 3 data, likely to enter the algorithm — included for positioning awareness only.

Obefazimod (ABX464)

Oral miR-124 enhancer · UC

Positive phase 3 induction and maintenance data. Abivax reported a positive pre-NDA interaction with the FDA in July 2026, with an NDA planned by end-2026. Oral once-daily; a genuinely novel mechanism, with activity reported in heavily treatment-experienced patients.

Tulisokibart (MK-7240)

Anti-TL1A monoclonal antibody · UC and CD

Met primary and key secondary endpoints in the phase 3 ATLAS-UC induction study (June 2026) — the first phase 3 success for the anti-TL1A class. Of particular interest for its potential antifibrotic effect and a candidate genetic biomarker for response.

Duvakitug

Anti-TL1A monoclonal antibody · UC and CD

Strong phase 2b results in both indications with durable efficacy reported; phase 3 programme ongoing. Same class as tulisokibart.

Risankizumab SC induction

Label expansion · CD

A fully subcutaneous induction regimen was submitted to regulators in 2026. If approved it would remove the IV induction requirement, matching guselkumab's all-SC option.
