Who to test

ACG frames this as a single decision, not two: "The determination of when to test for — and treat — H. pylori should be viewed as a single, rather than two separate and distinct, decisions." If you are not prepared to treat a positive result, do not send the test.

North American prevalence is 30–40% and falling, but concentrated in non-White populations, crowded or poor sanitary conditions, and early-generation immigrants from endemic regions. ACG 2024 · Key Concept 1

Peptic ulcer disease

Active or any past history

The oldest and strongest indication. A healed ulcer years ago still counts.

Gastric MALT lymphoma

Marginal zone B-cell lymphoma, MALT type

Eradication alone induces remission in a large majority of localised, low-grade disease.

Uninvestigated dyspepsia under 60

Test and treat, without endoscopy

In populations at high risk of gastric cancer, ACG moves the age down to 45–50.

Functional dyspepsia

Even after a normal endoscopy

A minority get durable symptomatic benefit, and eradication is worth doing on cancer-prevention grounds regardless.

Adult household members of a positive case

New in ACG 2024

Note the qualifier: the index case's positive test must be non-serological. Serology cannot establish active infection.

Long-term NSAIDs, or starting long-term low-dose aspirin

Before or during

Eradication reduces ulcer risk; it does not remove the need for a PPI in high-risk NSAID users.

Unexplained iron deficiency anaemia

After the usual GI workup

H. pylori impairs iron absorption and causes occult loss.

Idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura

Increased-risk populations

Certain non-White racial and ethnic groups · immigrants from high-incidence regions · hereditary cancer syndromes · autoimmune gastritis

First-line treatment

Four options, and only one carries a strong recommendation. All four run 14 days. The single biggest change from prior guidance is what is missing: PPI-clarithromycin triple therapy is no longer on this list.

What is no longer first-line

PPI + clarithromycin triple therapy

No longer a recommended first-line regimen

It does not appear in the ACG 2024 first-line table at all. The guideline says: "We advise against the use of clarithromycin in any treatment regimen unless there is evidence that a patient is infected with a clarithromycin-sensitive strain." The narrow residual role: if susceptibility is unknown, the patient has no history of macrolide use, and no alternative first-line therapy is available, it remains an option — but bismuth quadruple is recommended over it.

ACG 2024 · Key Concept 3

Concomitant (non-bismuth quadruple)

Not suggested over bismuth quadruple

PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin + metronidazole, all twice daily, 5–14 days. Rejected for lack of evidence of superiority and rising clarithromycin and metronidazole resistance. ACG 2024 prints no milligram doses for this regimen — it is incomplete as published.

ACG 2024 · Rec 5 · conditional, low

Levofloxacin regimens as first-line

Salvage only, and only with proven susceptibility

US levofloxacin resistance approaches 40%. It appears in ACG 2024 only as salvage.

ACG 2024 · Key Concept 3

Sequential and hybrid therapy

Dropped

Both were in the 2017 ACG/CAG guideline. Neither is carried into the 2024 regimen tables.

ACG 2024

Probiotics as an adjunct

Insufficient evidence

"There is insufficient evidence to suggest that the use of probiotic therapy improves the efficacy or tolerability of H. pylori eradication therapy."

ACG 2024 · Rec 12 · conditional, low

After a failed course

Salvage is decided by what the patient has already had, not by a fixed ladder. ACG gives no formal sequential algorithm beyond matching prior exposures.

The one rule that governs everything

Never reuse the class they have already seen

"A basic core principle for treating H. pylori infection is to avoid clarithromycin and levofloxacin in individuals who have had any exposure to, respectively, macrolides or fluoroquinolones." And: "It is no longer appropriate to use clarithromycin- or levofloxacin-containing treatment regimens empirically, especially in salvage therapy." Note "any exposure" — a course of azithromycin for a chest infection five years ago counts.

ACG 2024 · Key Concept 3

Failed a clarithromycin-containing regimen

Go to optimised bismuth quadruple

The most common salvage scenario, and the answer is the regimen that should usually have been first.

ACG 2024 · Rec 7 · conditional, low

Never had bismuth quadruple, whatever else failed

Optimised bismuth quadruple

Including in preference to quinolone-based therapy — stated as a separate recommendation.

ACG 2024 · Rec 6, 9 · conditional

Already failed bismuth quadruple

Rifabutin triple

Talicia at label dose, or generic components. ACG advises rifabutin 50 mg three times daily, or 150 mg twice daily if Talicia is unavailable — the latter is off-label. With a PPI at standard-to-double dose twice daily and amoxicillin 1,000 mg twice or three times daily, for 14 days.

ACG 2024 · Rec 8 · conditional, low

Levofloxacin triple

Only with proven susceptibility, and only after the others

PPI standard dose b.i.d. + levofloxacin 500 mg once daily + amoxicillin 1,000 mg b.i.d. (or metronidazole 500 mg b.i.d. if penicillin-allergic), 14 days. Reserved for patients with known levofloxacin-sensitive strains, when bismuth quadruple and rifabutin triple have been used or are unavailable.

ACG 2024 · Rec 10 · conditional, low

Confirmed clarithromycin-sensitive on testing

Then a clarithromycin triple is reasonable

PPI- or PCAB-based clarithromycin triple therapy is suggested where susceptibility has been demonstrated. This is the payoff for doing the susceptibility test.

ACG 2024 · Key Concept 5

High-dose PPI or PCAB dual as salvage

Not recommended — evidence gap

"There is insufficient evidence from North America to recommend high-dose PPI or PCAB dual therapy" in treatment-experienced patients. US data for omeprazole 40 mg t.i.d. plus amoxicillin reached only 57.7% (95% CI 51.2–64.0).

ACG 2024 · Rec 11 · no recommendation
The practical shape of it. Failed a clarithromycin regimen → bismuth quadruple. Failed bismuth quadruple → rifabutin triple. Levofloxacin only after both, and only with a susceptibility result in hand. If you are on a third attempt without having tested susceptibility, test before choosing.

Allergy, resistance and acid

Reported penicillin allergy

Start with bismuth quadruple

Of the four first-line regimens, only optimised bismuth quadruple is penicillin-free. That makes it the default in anyone with a reported penicillin allergy.

Then question the allergy

"When optimized BQT is not an appropriate option for a patient with suspected penicillin allergy, we suggest referral to an allergist to confirm a true allergy and to consider penicillin desensitization, as <1% of the population has true type 1 IgE-mediated allergy to penicillins." Most reported penicillin allergy is not allergy. Delabelling opens up three of the four first-line regimens.

If true allergy is confirmed

Bismuth quadruple first. For salvage, the penicillin-free levofloxacin variant substitutes metronidazole 500 mg twice daily for amoxicillin — and still requires proven levofloxacin susceptibility.

Where Maastricht VI differs

Maastricht VI also puts bismuth quadruple first, but routes failures to a fluoroquinolone-containing regimen rather than to allergy evaluation and desensitisation. ACG's referral-to-allergist step is the more useful move, because it can restore the whole first-line menu.

ACG 2024 says "referral to an allergist" and "penicillin desensitization". It does not use the terms skin testing or direct oral challenge, though ACG/CAG 2017 did recommend re-evaluating the allergy label before excluding amoxicillin regimens. ACG 2024

Susceptibility testing

ACG position — selective, not routine

Test when the choice is unclear

"The incremental benefit of selecting an eradication regimen tailored to the antibiotic susceptibility profile compared with empiric selection remains to be adequately defined and studied. Based on expert consensus, we advise using antibiotic susceptibility testing whenever the choice of therapy remains unclear after taking into consideration any previous treatments for H. pylori infection, past antibiotic exposure more generally, and whether there is a documented history of penicillin allergy."

ACG 2024 · Key Concept 6

But it is mandatory before clarithromycin or levofloxacin

Key Concept 3 is the binding constraint

Those two drugs should be avoided absent demonstrated susceptibility. So while ACG does not require testing before every first-line regimen, it does require it before the two regimens most people reach for by habit.

ACG 2024 · Key Concept 3

How it is done

Phenotypic or molecular

Culture-based phenotypic testing, or molecular methods — PCR or next-generation sequencing — on gastric biopsy or stool. Molecular stool testing is increasingly available in the US and does not require endoscopy.

ACG 2024 · Key Concept 6

US resistance rates

The numbers that justify the whole restructuring

Clarithromycin 20–30% (pooled 2011–2021 prevalence 31.5%). Levofloxacin approaching 40%. ACG: these rates "equate to predicted eradication success of 70% or less when used empirically."

ACG 2024

Where Maastricht VI disagrees

Europe says test everyone, first

"It is reasonable to recommend that susceptibility tests (molecular or after culture) are routinely performed, even before prescribing first-line treatment, in respect to antibiotic stewardship." ACG explicitly declines to require this, calling the incremental benefit undefined. A real transatlantic split.

Getting the acid suppression right

CYP2C19 genotype

Choose the PPI that does not care

"In patients known to have the rapid or ultrarapid CYP2C19 genotype, we suggest esomeprazole or rabeprazole as the preferred PPI." If using omeprazole, lansoprazole or pantoprazole in such patients, increase the dose. Vonoprazan is not a CYP2C19 substrate at all — a stated advantage.

ACG 2024

PPI timing

30–60 minutes before a meal

Printed as a footnote to the regimen table and routinely ignored in practice. Acid suppression is the scaffolding the antibiotics stand on.

ACG 2024 · Table 5

Dose escalation

Only for the weaker PPIs

ACG endorses raising the dose of CYP2C19-dependent PPIs, but declines to recommend high-dose PPI dual therapy as a regimen. Maastricht VI goes further, stating high-dose twice-daily PPI increases the efficacy of triple therapy, while it remains unclear whether it helps quadruple therapies.

ACG 2024Maastricht VI

Adherence

Say why, and say what will happen

"To maximize adherence, patients should be educated on the reason it is important to treat H. pylori and the most frequent adverse effects that could occur with treatment." Four-times-daily bismuth quadruple over 14 days is a demanding schedule; a patient who stops on day 6 because of nausea has been given a resistant organism, not a cure.

ACG 2024

Testing and test of cure

Everyone gets a test of cure

"All patients who are treated for H. pylori infection should undergo a test of cure with an appropriately conducted urea breath test, fecal antigen test, or biopsy-based test at least 4 weeks after completion of therapy." No exceptions in the guideline. Given first-line eradication rates in the high 70s to 80s, roughly one patient in five is still infected — and asymptomatic.

ACG 2024 · Key Concept 4

The two washouts that invalidate the result

PPI: stop 2 weeks before testing. Bismuth and antibiotics: stop 4 weeks before. Both cause false negatives. H2 blockers and antacids at standard doses do not affect accuracy — use them to cover symptoms during the washout.

ACG 2024

Timing

At least 4 weeks after finishing treatment, with the acid suppression stopped for at least the last 2 of those weeks. In practice: finish therapy, stop the PPI at week 2, test at week 4.

ACG 2024

Vonoprazan washout is unknown

ACG assumes the same considerations apply to PCABs but states plainly that this has not been adequately studied. No verified hold period exists for vonoprazan.

After eradication

Gastric cancer surveillance

ACG acknowledges it and defers

"Endoscopic surveillance is indicated in patients with high-risk gastric premalignant conditions because these lesions can still progress despite successful eradication." But the guideline gives no intervals and no protocol, directing readers to other guidance documents without naming them. Do not expect an answer here.

ACG 2024not in guideline

The risk numbers

What is being surveilled for

10-year cumulative risk of progression from gastric intestinal metaplasia to cancer 1.6% (95% CI 1.5–1.7). In high-risk GIM the risk is 2 to 4.5 times greater. High-risk features: corpus-extended GIM, incomplete GIM, and dysplasia, plus family history, birth in a high-incidence country, and high-risk race or ethnicity.

ACG 2024

Atrophic gastritis can regress

A reason to eradicate even late

"Mild to moderate atrophic gastritis may be reversible in some patients after successful H. pylori eradication." Intestinal metaplasia is generally not.

About this page

Adult dosing, normal renal and hepatic function.

Clinical decision support, not a prescribing authority. Verify every dose against the current local label before use. Paediatric, pregnancy, renal and hepatic dosing are not covered, and regional availability of tetracycline, bismuth salts, rifabutin and vonoprazan varies widely.

Where the guidelines disagree

Susceptibility testing. Maastricht VI: routinely, even before first-line therapy, on antibiotic-stewardship grounds. ACG 2024: only when the choice of therapy is unclear. guidelines differ
Clarithromycin triple. Maastricht VI still permits it first-line where local clarithromycin resistance is under 15% and it is proven effective locally. ACG 2024 removes it from the first-line table entirely. guidelines differ
Label versus guideline. Voquezna TriplePak is FDA-approved without any susceptibility-testing requirement, while ACG Key Concept 3 says avoid clarithromycin absent demonstrated susceptibility. Prescribing strictly to label can conflict with the guideline. guidelines differ
Rifabutin dosing. Talicia's label is fixed at rifabutin 50 mg every 8 hours for 14 days. ACG's salvage table permits 50–300 mg in various schedules, advising 50 mg three times daily or 150 mg twice daily if Talicia is unavailable — the latter is off-label. guidelines differ
Bismuth salt dose. ACG's "optimised" definition says 300 mg four times daily at minimum, while its tables also print bismuth subcitrate 120–300 mg q.i.d. Canadian commentary on the same guideline uses bismuth subsalicylate 524 mg q.i.d. Absolute bismuth exposure therefore varies between sources. guidelines differ

What ACG 2024 does not answer

The guideline names "standard dose" PPI throughout but never defines it in milligrams for any individual PPI. It gives no milligram doses at all for concomitant therapy. It gives no post-eradication surveillance intervals, deferring to unnamed other documents. And it states that the vonoprazan washout before a test of cure has not been adequately studied. These are gaps in the source, not omissions on this page.
