Pancreatic Cysts
Kyoto 2024
Searches the cyst type cards
Start with the type, not the size. Imaging alone gets the cyst type right only 40–50% of the time, and benign versus malignant only 55–76%. The type determines whether size even matters — a 4 cm serous cystadenoma needs nothing, a 2 cm main-duct IPMN needs a surgeon.
MRI with MRCP is the imaging of choice — no radiation, and it shows communication with the pancreatic duct, which is the single most useful discriminating feature. CT and EUS are good alternatives where MRI is not possible.
Branch-duct IPMN the commonest incidental cyst — mucinous, premalignant Communicates with the duct · Surveillance by size
Who
Both sexes, typically 60s–70s. Anywhere, but often head and uncinate. Frequently multifocal — which is a feature of the disease, not multiple diseases.
Imaging
Communication with the pancreatic duct is the defining feature and the reason MRCP is the imaging of choice. Classically a "cluster of grapes" — a multilocular cyst without a thick wall. Main duct is normal or under 5 mm.
Cyst fluid
CEA high · glucose low · amylase high (because it communicates with the duct) · KRAS and GNAS mutations — GNAS is essentially specific for IPMN.
Management
Risk-stratify against high-risk stigmata and worrisome features, then surveil or resect. This is the lesion the whole Risk and Surveillance tabs are about.
Main-duct IPMN main duct dilatation without another cause Malignancy risk 33–85% · Resect if fit
Who
Both sexes, 60s–70s. Segmental or diffuse.
Imaging
Main pancreatic duct ≥ 5 mm with no obstructing cause. A duct of ≥ 10 mm is a high-risk stigma on its own. Look for a patulous papilla oozing mucin at endoscopy — pathognomonic.
Cyst fluid
As for branch-duct IPMN, though fluid analysis rarely changes the plan here.
Management
Resection if the patient is fit for surgery — European guidelines and Kyoto agree. The malignancy risk is an order of magnitude above branch-duct disease. Mixed-type carries the same risk as main-duct and is managed the same way.
Mucinous cystic neoplasm MCN — ovarian-type stroma, premalignant Almost always women · Resect if ≥ 4 cm or any risk feature
Who
Over 95% women, typically 40s–50s. Body and tail in about 95%. Usually solitary.
Imaging
A single unilocular or few-locular cyst with a thick wall, sometimes with peripheral eggshell calcification — which is highly suggestive and also associated with malignancy. No communication with the pancreatic duct — this is what separates it from IPMN.
Cyst fluid
CEA high · glucose low · amylase low (no ductal communication) · KRAS, but GNAS negative.
Management
European guidance: resect if ≥ 40 mm, or at any size with risk features or symptoms. Under 40 mm without risk features can be followed. Between 30 and 40 mm, weigh age, comorbidity and preference. Surveillance: 6-monthly for a year, then annually.

Once resected without cancer, no further surveillance is needed — unlike IPMN.
Serous cystadenoma SCA — benign, almost never needs anything Benign · Symptom-based follow-up after 1 year
Who
Women more than men, 60s. Anywhere.
Imaging
Microcystic honeycomb of many small cysts, often with a central stellate scar and calcification. A macrocystic variant exists and is the one mistaken for a mucinous cyst. No ductal communication.
Cyst fluid
CEA low (typically under 5 ng/mL) · glucose high · amylase low · VHL mutation, no KRAS or GNAS.
Management
Benign, with near-zero malignant potential. Once the diagnosis is confident, European guidance recommends confirming at 1 year and then symptom-based follow-up only. Surgery only for symptomatic compression. ACG: no follow-up needed after resection.
Solid pseudopapillary neoplasm SPN — low-grade malignant, young women Resect all · 5 years of follow-up
Who
Young women, 20s–30s. Any location.
Imaging
A well-encapsulated mass with mixed solid and cystic components and haemorrhagic degeneration — the haemorrhage is characteristic.
Cyst fluid
Cytology is usually diagnostic. β-catenin mutation.
Management
Radical resection for all, regardless of size — and an aggressive surgical approach even with advanced disease, because outcomes after complete resection are excellent. ACG: yearly follow-up for at least 5 years after resection.
Cystic neuroendocrine tumour the mimic that is often missed Not a benign cyst · Treat as a NET
Who
Any age or sex. Consider MEN1 in younger or multifocal cases.
Imaging
A hypervascular enhancing rim with a cystic centre, no ductal communication. Arterial-phase enhancement is the clue that this is not a mucinous cyst.
Cyst fluid
Chromogranin A and synaptophysin on cytology. CEA low.
Management
Managed as a neuroendocrine tumour, not as a pancreatic cyst — staging, grading and resection decisions follow NET guidance.
Pseudocyst inflammatory, not neoplastic History of pancreatitis · Amylase very high
Who
A history of acute or chronic pancreatitis, or of pancreatic trauma. Without that history, question the diagnosis.
Imaging
A unilocular collection with a well-defined non-epithelialised wall, at least 4 weeks after an episode of pancreatitis. May communicate with the duct.
Cyst fluid
Amylase very high · CEA low · glucose high. Amylase under 250 U/L excludes a pseudocyst in 98% of cases — a genuinely useful rule-out.
Management
Drain only if symptomatic, infected or causing obstruction. Most resolve. The trap is labelling a mucinous neoplasm a pseudocyst on the basis of a remote or unconvincing history of pancreatitis.

Cyst fluid & EUS-FNA

Aspirate only when the result would change what you do. A cyst already destined for surgery, and a cyst in a patient who would never have surgery, both fail that test.

When to do EUS ± FNA
  • The diagnosis is unclear and the result would alter management
  • Any concerning feature — mural nodule, solid component, main duct > 5 mm, size ≥ 3 cm, jaundice, cyst-related pancreatitis, raised CA 19-9Kyoto: EUS is specifically recommended when there is suspicion of high-grade dysplasia or invasive cancer
  • To obtain cytology when imaging alone is not enough to justify surgery
Do not aspirate a cyst that already meets criteria for resection, a confidently diagnosed serous cystadenoma, or a cyst in someone who is not a surgical candidate. Also weigh the small but real risks — pancreatitis, bleeding, infection.

What each test tells you

Glucose < 50 mg/dL
Mucinous — the best single test
Sensitivity 93%, specificity 89% for distinguishing mucinous from non-mucinous. Outperforms CEA, costs almost nothing, and can be run on a bedside glucometer with a tiny volume. If you order one fluid test, order this one.
CEA > 192 ng/mL
Mucinous — the traditional test
Sensitivity only 58–63%, specificity 87–93%. Levels above 800 ng/mL push specificity beyond 95%. CEA cannot identify dysplasia or cancer — a high CEA says "mucinous", not "malignant", and a low CEA does not exclude malignancy.
Amylase < 250 U/L
Excludes a pseudocyst
Excludes pseudocyst in 98% of cases. High amylase indicates ductal communication — expected in IPMN and pseudocyst, not in MCN or serous cystadenoma.
KRAS and/or GNAS mutation
Mucinous — molecular
Sensitivity 79%, specificity 98%. GNAS is essentially specific for IPMN and helps separate it from MCN. Useful when the diagnosis would change management and imaging plus routine fluid analysis has not settled it.
TP53, SMAD4, CDKN2A or PIK3CA
Advanced neoplasia
Sensitivity only 9–39% but specificity 92–98%. A positive result is meaningful; a negative one is not reassuring. These point to high-grade dysplasia or invasive cancer rather than merely to a mucinous lesion.
Cytology
The only test that grades
The only fluid test that can demonstrate high-grade dysplasia or carcinoma. Low sensitivity because of scant cellularity, but a positive result is a high-risk stigma in its own right and mandates surgery.
The distinction that matters most is mucinous versus non-mucinous, because only mucinous cysts — IPMN and MCN — are premalignant. Glucose has largely replaced CEA as the first-line fluid test: better sensitivity, negligible cost, tiny volume. CEA remains useful as a confirmatory second marker.

Risk features

Three guidelines, three vocabularies, largely overlapping content. Kyoto's two-tier system is the international standard for IPMN; the European absolute-versus-relative framing is the most useful when talking to a surgeon.

Kyoto 2024 — high-risk stigmata

Any one → surgery, after excluding mimics
  • Obstructive jaundice with a cystic lesion in the pancreatic head
  • Enhancing mural nodule ≥ 5 mm, or a solid componentMeasured on contrast-enhanced EUS or MRI
  • Main pancreatic duct ≥ 10 mm
  • Suspicious or positive cytologyIf FNA was performed

Kyoto 2024 — worrisome features

Any one → EUS and multidisciplinary assessment
  • Acute pancreatitis attributable to the cyst
  • Elevated serum CA 19-9Without another explanation
  • New-onset diabetes, or acute worsening within the past yearA Kyoto addition — worth asking about directly
  • Cyst size ≥ 30 mm
  • Enhancing mural nodule < 5 mm
  • Thickened or enhancing cyst walls
  • Main pancreatic duct 5–9 mm
  • Abrupt change in duct calibre with distal parenchymal atrophy
  • Lymphadenopathy
  • Growth rate ≥ 2.5 mm/yearKyoto lowered this from 5 mm/year
Worrisome features are cumulative. One in an elderly comorbid patient may justify continued surveillance; two or three in a fit patient shift the balance decisively toward resection. Kyoto explicitly invokes nomograms and multidisciplinary judgement rather than a fixed count.

How the three frameworks compare

Kyoto 2024 — international consensus

High-risk stigmata → surgery after excluding mimics. Worrisome features → EUS, nomogram assessment, then surveillance or surgery by clinical judgement. Neither → surveillance by size.

The current international standard for IPMN, replacing the 2017 Fukuoka revision. Notable changes: new-onset diabetes added as a worrisome feature, and the growth-rate threshold lowered to 2.5 mm/year.

ACG 2018 — concerning features

Jaundice · acute pancreatitis from the cyst · raised CA 19-9 · mural nodule or solid component · main duct > 5 mm · change in duct calibre with upstream atrophy · size ≥ 3 cm · growth ≥ 3 mm/year · high-grade dysplasia or cancer on cytology.

A single flat list rather than two tiers. Any concerning feature prompts EUS ± FNA and multidisciplinary referral. Note the main-duct threshold is 5 mm rather than Kyoto's two-tier 5–9 and ≥ 10.

European 2018 — absolute vs relative

Absolute indications for surgery: positive cytology for malignancy or high-grade dysplasia · solid mass · tumour-related jaundice · enhancing mural nodule ≥ 5 mm · main duct ≥ 10 mm.

Relative indications: growth ≥ 5 mm/year · CA 19-9 > 37 U/mL · main duct 5–9.9 mm · cyst ≥ 40 mm · new-onset diabetes · acute pancreatitis · enhancing mural nodule < 5 mm.

The cleanest framing for a surgical conversation: absolute indications operate, relative indications trigger a weighted discussion — and two or more relative indications shift most patients toward resection.

MRI with MRCP is the surveillance modality across all three guidelines — no radiation over what may be decades of imaging, and the best view of the duct. EUS is added when a mural nodule needs characterising.
The schedules differ more in style than in substance. Kyoto front-loads with early repeat imaging then lengthens; ACG sets fixed blocks of years and then lengthens if stable; Europe uses a simple 6 months then annual. Pick the one your service has adopted and apply it consistently — switching between them mid-surveillance is how intervals get lost.

When surveillance can stop

Stable branch-duct IPMN under 20 mm after 5 years

Kyoto permits either stopping or continuing — no morphological change and no worrisome features after five years of surveillance. The argument for continuing is the separate risk of concomitant pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma arising elsewhere in the gland, which is independent of the cyst itself and does not disappear with time.

No longer a surgical candidate

Stop. ACG is explicit: surveillance should be discontinued if the patient would not undergo surgery whatever is found. Continuing generates anxiety, cost and incidental findings with no possible benefit. This is the most commonly ignored recommendation in the whole area.

Age over 75

ACG suggests it is reasonable to reassess the utility of surveillance. Between 76 and 85, individualise. The question is not the age but whether the patient would accept and survive a pancreatectomy.

Serous cystadenoma

Confirm the diagnosis at 1 year, then symptom-based follow-up only.

Surgery & after

Resect

Any high-risk stigma or absolute indication. All main-duct and mixed-type IPMN in a fit patient. All solid pseudopapillary neoplasms. MCN ≥ 40 mm, or smaller with risk features or symptoms. Any cyst with positive cytology for high-grade dysplasia or carcinoma.

Discuss

Worrisome features or relative indications — weigh number of features, age, comorbidity, life expectancy, the operative risk of the specific resection, and the patient's own preference. A distal pancreatectomy in a fit 55-year-old and a Whipple in a frail 80-year-old are not the same conversation.

Do not operate

Confident serous cystadenoma without symptoms. Pseudocyst without complications. Any cyst in a patient unfit for pancreatic surgery — and in that case, stop surveillance too.

Before committing to surgery, Kyoto asks explicitly for counselling about intraoperative margin assessment and the possibility of proceeding to total pancreatectomy — a conversation that has to happen before the anaesthetic, not during it.

After resection

Resected IPMN

All resected IPMN require postoperative surveillance — remnant gland recurrence and metachronous lesions are well described, including after negative margins.

Resected MCN without cancer

No postoperative surveillance needed. Complete resection is curative.

Serous cystadenoma, pseudocyst, other benign cyst

No follow-up required.

Solid pseudopapillary neoplasm

Yearly follow-up for at least 5 years.

Invasive carcinoma in any cyst

Oncological follow-up per pancreatic cancer protocols.

Two things these guidelines exclude

Familial pancreatic cancer and known germline mutations. The ACG guideline states plainly that it does not apply to patients with a strong family history of pancreatic cancer or a predisposing genetic mutation — BRCA1/2, PALB2, ATM, CDKN2A, STK11, Lynch syndrome, hereditary pancreatitis. Those patients follow dedicated high-risk surveillance protocols, usually annual EUS and MRI at a specialist centre, and a cyst in that setting is interpreted quite differently.
Concomitant pancreatic cancer. Patients with IPMN carry an elevated risk of ductal adenocarcinoma arising elsewhere in the gland, separate from the cyst. It is the main argument for continuing surveillance in cysts that have been stable for years, and the reason a new symptom in a patient with a known stable cyst deserves fresh imaging rather than reassurance.

Quick answers

Which single cyst fluid test should I order?
AnswerGlucose. Under 50 mg/dL means mucinous — 93% sensitive, 89% specific, better than CEA, and it runs on a glucometer.
Does a high CEA mean cancer?
AnswerNo. It means mucinous. CEA cannot grade dysplasia at all — only cytology can.
Which feature distinguishes IPMN from MCN?
AnswerCommunication with the pancreatic duct — present in IPMN, absent in MCN. Hence high amylase in IPMN, low in MCN. GNAS mutation also favours IPMN.
A woman in her 40s with a single thick-walled cyst in the tail?
AnswerMCN until proven otherwise — over 95% are women and about 95% are in the body or tail.
Does main-duct IPMN need surgery?
AnswerYes if the patient is fit — malignancy risk is far above branch-duct disease. Mixed-type is treated the same.
Does a resected MCN need follow-up?
AnswerNo, if there was no invasive cancer. Resected IPMN always does.
Can I stop surveillance on a stable small cyst?
AnswerKyoto allows stopping a stable branch-duct IPMN under 20 mm after 5 years — or continuing, because of the separate risk of cancer elsewhere in the gland. Definitely stop if the patient would not have surgery.
What changed in Kyoto 2024?
AnswerNew-onset or worsening diabetes became a worrisome feature, and the growth threshold dropped to 2.5 mm/year from 5. Surveillance was restructured into three size bands.

About this page

Clinical decision support, not a prescribing authority. Adults with incidentally detected pancreatic cystic lesions. Does not apply to patients with a strong family history of pancreatic cancer or a known predisposing germline mutation — they follow dedicated high-risk protocols.
Why three guidelines are shown. Kyoto 2024 is the international consensus for IPMN and supersedes Fukuoka 2017. ACG 2018 and the European 2018 guidelines remain widely used and differ in thresholds and in surveillance schedules. Where they disagree, all positions are shown rather than one being silently chosen.
