Staging without a needle

The Baveno consensus replaced "compensated cirrhosis" with compensated advanced chronic liver disease — cACLD — because advanced fibrosis and early cirrhosis cannot reliably be separated in practice, and the management is the same. The question that matters is not the histology but whether there is clinically significant portal hypertension.

CSPH — clinically significant portal hypertension

HVPG ≥ 10 mmHg

The threshold above which varices develop, decompensation becomes likely and HCC risk rises. HVPG ≥ 12 mmHg is the threshold for variceal bleeding; ≥ 20 mmHg measured during an acute bleed identifies those who will fail standard treatment and should have a pre-emptive TIPS.

Liver stiffness — the rule of five

Transient elastography, fasted, in a patient without acute hepatitis, cholestasis, congestion or heavy alcohol use in the preceding days — all of which raise stiffness independently of fibrosis. Baveno VIII keeps the rule of five (10–15–20–25 kPa) as a marker of progressively higher risk of decompensation and liver-related death, independent of aetiology.

Following the numbers over time in steatotic liver disease

Panel 2 is new in Baveno VIII and it answers a question the earlier consensus never did: what counts as a real change when you repeat the test. The thresholds differ by modality, and the reason for repeating has shifted.

The point of the test has changed

Calibrate non-invasive tests, and their changes over time, to estimate the risk of liver-related events — not to assign or track a fibrosis stage. This is the same shift as statement 1.18, stated as a rule for how to use the numbers. A patient whose stiffness falls by a third has a different prognosis; whether that equals one METAVIR stage is the wrong question.

Baveno VIII 2.8

MASLD: HVPG may under-read the pressure

In MASLD-related cACLD, HVPG may underestimate the true portal pressure gradient — a genuine measurement caveat, not a rounding issue. An HVPG of 10 mmHg or above still carries a significantly increased risk of decompensation, but the risk below 10 mmHg is lower rather than absent. Do not read a sub-threshold HVPG in MASLD as an all-clear.

Baveno VIII 2.5

MASLD without CSPH: what a falling stiffness means

A confirmed ≥ 30% relative reduction in LSM may indicate regression of at least one fibrosis stage. Pairing it with an independent test — an ELF fall of at least 0.5 units — assesses that better than LSM alone.

Baveno VIII 2.6–2.7

Weight loss moves the number

A 10% or greater weight reduction is associated with a ≥ 30% relative reduction in LSM — which is itself a surrogate for reduced risk of liver-related events. This is the mechanism by which lifestyle change shows up on the monitoring you are already doing.

Baveno VIII 2.13

Alcohol: the driver, and the tests that track it

Return to or continuation of alcohol use is a major driver of liver-related events in alcohol-related cACLD. FIB-4, LSM, the ELF test and Fibrotest are all associated with liver-related events and survival in alcohol-related liver disease — so you have more than one way to follow these patients.

Who to treat

cACLD with CSPH — varices or no varices

Carvedilol, to prevent decompensation and improve survival

The central shift of the modern Baveno consensus, and Baveno VIII states it as the headline indication: NSBBs, particularly carvedilol, are recommended in compensated cirrhosis or cACLD with CSPH, to prevent decompensation and improve survival. Following PREDESCI, beta blockers are no longer prescribed only to protect against variceal bleeding. The indication is the portal pressure, not the varices.

Baveno VIII 3.15

cACLD without CSPH

Beta blockers should not be used

An explicit negative recommendation. They do not prevent decompensation or death in the absence of CSPH, and simply add side effects.

Baveno VIII 3.16

Gastric varices present

They imply CSPH — treat accordingly

Patients with compensated cirrhosis or cACLD who have gastric varices have CSPH by definition and benefit from an NSBB, particularly carvedilol. You do not need to prove the pressure separately.

Baveno VIII 3.21a

Contraindication or intolerance to a beta blocker

Endoscopy, then band the large varices

Do the endoscopy, and if large oesophageal or GOV1 varices are found, band them to prevent a first bleed. EVL is the fallback, not the first choice — it protects against bleeding but does nothing for portal pressure or for ascites.

Baveno VIII 3.19

Ascites already present, with varices

Carvedilol or an NSBB should be used — carvedilol preferred

Decompensation does not remove the indication. Carvedilol remains preferred provided the blood pressure tolerates it.

Baveno VIII 4.4

The drugs

Baveno states no beta blocker doses. Panel 3 names carvedilol as the preferred agent and gives the indication, but no milligrams, no titration rule and no heart-rate or blood-pressure target. The doses below are the trial and practice regimens.
Carvedilol preferred agent 6.25 mg daily · max 12.5 mg/day
Dose
Start 3.125–6.25 mg once daily, titrate after a few days to 12.5 mg/day — once daily in the trials, though splitting into 6.25 mg twice daily is often better tolerated.
Titration target
Do not chase a heart rate target with carvedilol. Titrate against blood pressure and tolerance. Keep systolic BP above 90 mmHg and MAP above 65 mmHg.
Why this one
Blocks β-receptors and α1-receptors, so it lowers intrahepatic vascular resistance as well as portal inflow. That gives a greater HVPG reduction than propranolol at equivalent β-blockade, and it works in some patients who do not respond to traditional NSBBs.
CautionIts greater blood-pressure effect is the price. Avoid or reduce in refractory ascites with low blood pressure — propranolol may be better tolerated there. Carvedilol is advised against in decompensated disease with hypotension.
Propranolol traditional non-selective beta blocker 20–40 mg twice daily · max 320 mg/day
Dose
Start 20–40 mg twice daily, titrating every few days. Maximum 320 mg/day, reduced to 160 mg/day if ascites is present.
Titration target
Titrate to the maximum tolerated dose, aiming for a resting heart rate around 55–60 bpm while keeping systolic BP above 90 mmHg.
Why this one
Long track record and cheap. A reasonable choice when carvedilol lowers the blood pressure too far.
CautionHeart-rate response correlates poorly with HVPG response, so a patient at target heart rate is not necessarily protected. Asthma, high-degree AV block and severe peripheral arterial disease are contraindications. EASL advises avoiding doses above 80 mg/day in severe or refractory ascites.
Nadolol once-daily alternative 20–40 mg daily · max 160 mg/day
Dose
Start 20–40 mg once daily. Maximum 160 mg/day, halved to 80 mg/day with ascites.
Titration target
As for propranolol — heart rate 55–60 bpm, systolic BP above 90 mmHg.
Why this one

Everything else that has been tried

Statins

Baveno VIII steps back

Baveno VII encouraged statins, noting they may lower portal pressure and improve survival, capping simvastatin at 20 mg/day in Child-Pugh B and C. Baveno VIII does not recommend statins for the prevention of further decompensation. This does not touch their cardiovascular indication — if the patient needs a statin for their heart, they still need it.

Now recommended against

Aetiological cure

Outranks every drug here

Alcohol abstinence, sustained HBV suppression, HCV SVR, weight loss and metabolic control in MASLD. Removing the cause lowers portal pressure, can reverse cACLD, and is the only intervention that changes the trajectory rather than managing its consequences.

Baveno VIII 7.5

Proton pump inhibitors

Withdraw unless there is a clear indication

New in Baveno VIII. PPIs are continued reflexively after a variceal bleed and rarely reviewed; they are associated with infection and encephalopathy in cirrhosis.

Baveno VIII 6.24

Treating the steatotic liver disease itself

Panel 2 of Baveno VIII. The strongest statements here are the negative ones — what not to extrapolate, and what not to settle for.

Complete abstinence, not reduction

In alcohol-related cACLD

Complete alcohol abstinence should be favoured over reduction of consumption to decrease liver-related events. Baveno VIII states this at 100% agreement. "Cutting down" is not the goal being recommended.

Baveno VIII 2.2

SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1-based therapies

Safe to use in cACLD

SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1-based therapies — mono- and multi-receptor agonists — can be safely used in patients with cACLD, absent contraindications, for their approved indications: type 2 diabetes, and for the GLP-1-based agents, obesity. A clear green light where practice has often been hesitant.

Baveno VIII 2.19

No approved drug for MASLD with established cirrhosis

And do not extrapolate

There are no regulatory-approved pharmacological therapies for MASLD with established cirrhosis. Efficacy data from non-cirrhotic MASLD populations with moderate or advanced fibrosis should not be extrapolated to patients who already have cirrhosis. Management focuses on lifestyle, metabolic comorbidities, preventing decompensation, and referral to clinical trials.

Baveno VIII 2.17–2.18

Metabolic and bariatric surgery — without CSPH

A lower BMI threshold is justified

In MASLD-related cACLD without CSPH, surgery carries only a modest increase in complications and may improve liver histology and liver-related events. So MASLD-related cACLD may itself be considered an obesity-associated morbidity that justifies considering surgery at a lower BMI threshold than you otherwise would.

Baveno VIII 2.14

Metabolic and bariatric surgery — with CSPH

Expert centre, multidisciplinary team

Surgical complications are increased once CSPH is present. Patients with features suggesting CSPH who are being considered for surgery should be managed by a multidisciplinary team at an expert centre.

Baveno VIII 2.15

Metabolic and bariatric surgery — decompensated

Generally contraindicated

Except in highly specialised settings, such as in conjunction with liver transplantation, and again only under a multidisciplinary team at an expert centre.

Baveno VIII 2.16

Acute variceal bleeding

Mortality is around 15–20% at six weeks. Most of what determines the outcome happens in the first few hours, before and around the endoscopy — not at the endoscopy itself.

Minute 0 — before anything else
  • Vasoactive drug immediately, on suspicion, before endoscopy. Terlipressin, somatostatin or octreotide, started as soon as possible and continued 2–5 days, or until a TIPS is placed if one is indicated. Usual regimens: terlipressin 2 mg IV every 4 h for the first 48 h then 1 mg every 4 h; somatostatin 250 µg bolus then 250–500 µg/h; octreotide 50 µg bolus then 50 µg/h. Baveno VIII 5.7
  • A 24-hour course may be enough after successful endoscopic haemostasis, provided a beta blocker is started afterwards — new in Baveno VIII, and a real change from running the drip for five days by default. Baveno VIII 5.8
  • Check the sodium on terlipressin. Hyponatraemia can be profound and rapid, particularly in younger patients with preserved liver function. Baveno VIII 5.9
  • Antibiotic prophylaxis — not optional. It reduces infection, rebleeding and mortality. Third-generation cephalosporins are generally preferred; ceftriaxone 1 g IV daily is the usual choice, adjusted to local resistance. Baveno VIII 5.11
  • Duration is now individualised rather than a fixed 7 days, and Baveno VIII allows that Child-Pugh A patients may need a shorter course or none at all. AASLD and EASL still specify a maximum of 7 days. Baveno VIII 5.10guidelines differ
  • Restrictive transfusion, target haemoglobin 7–8 g/dL. Baveno VIII narrows the old 7–9 window, while allowing individualisation for cardiovascular status, age, haemodynamic stability and ongoing bleeding. Over-transfusion raises portal pressure and provokes rebleeding. Baveno VIII 5.4
  • Careful crystalloid resuscitation — enough for perfusion, not enough to over-expand.
  • No fresh frozen plasma — it does not correct the coagulopathy of cirrhosis and is associated with worse outcomes. No routine platelet transfusion. INR is not a measure of bleeding risk here and must not delay endoscopy.
Before the scope
  • Erythromycin 250 mg IV, 30–90 minutes before endoscopy, to clear the stomach. Improves visualisation and reduces transfusion requirement. Baveno VIII 5.20
  • Intubate before endoscopy for altered consciousness or active haematemesis — and extubate promptly afterwards. Routine intubation causes pneumonia. Baveno VIII 5.5
  • Lactulose, oral or by enema, to prevent or treat encephalopathy by clearing blood from the gut. Baveno VIII 5.22
  • Start a PPI, but as a short course — its role is post-banding ulceration, not the bleeding itself. Review it for withdrawal afterwards.
Within 12 hours — endoscopy
  • Within 12 hours of presentation once haemodynamically stable; as soon as possible if unstable. Baveno VIII 5.18
  • Oesophageal varices and GOV1: band ligation, first-line. Place 5–10 bands, starting at the bleeding point and then spiralling upward from the gastro-oesophageal junction. More than six bands adds nothing. Baveno VIII 5.29
  • GOV2 and IGV1 — the plan has changed. Cyanoacrylate injection or EUS-guided therapy for immediate haemostasis, but the preferred definitive treatment is TIPS with or without variceal embolisation, as soon as possible. Baveno VII treated glue as the destination; Baveno VIII treats it as the bridge. Baveno VIII 5.40–5.41
  • Record whether there was active bleeding at endoscopy — that single observation decides who gets a pre-emptive TIPS.
  • A pre-existing portal vein thrombosis does not change the acute management: treat the bleed exactly as you would without it. Baveno VIII 5.45
Then — decide about TIPS
  • Assess Child-Pugh class and whether bleeding was active at endoscopy. See the TIPS tab.
  • Do not wait to see whether the patient rebleeds. The whole point of pre-emptive TIPS is that it is done before failure, not after.
Terlipressin cautions. Check the sodium — it causes hyponatraemia, sometimes profound and rapid, particularly in younger patients with preserved liver function. Avoid in significant ischaemic heart disease or peripheral vascular disease; watch for skin and bowel ischaemia. Reduce with renal impairment.
Not every upper GI bleed in a cirrhotic patient is variceal. Roughly a third are not. Treat as variceal until the endoscopy says otherwise — the drugs and antibiotics do no harm meanwhile — but do not stop looking once the varices are banded if the picture does not fit.

The other sources of portal hypertensive bleeding

Ectopic varices

Endovascular or endoscopic

Either endovascular treatment — TIPS or transvenous obliteration — or endoscopic treatment should be considered. TIPS may be combined with embolisation, particularly when portal flow stays diverted to collaterals despite a fall in the portosystemic gradient.

Baveno VIII 5.42

Transvenous obliteration

When TIPS is contraindicated

For gastric varices, transvenous obliteration (BRTO and its variants) is an option where TIPS cannot be done, provided the shunt anatomy is suitable and local expertise exists. Remember it raises portal pressure and can worsen oesophageal varices and ascites.

Baveno VIII 5.41

Portal hypertensive gastropathy

Lower the pressure — not the same disease as GAVE

Management focuses on reducing portal pressure with an NSBB. Endoscopic therapy offers limited benefit except for focal bleeding or as salvage. For severe or refractory PHG bleeding, TIPS is the most effective rescue therapy.

Baveno VIII 5.44

GAVE

Band it — Baveno VIII now says EVL over APC

A real change. Baveno VII only flagged that GAVE must be distinguished from PHG, without recommending a treatment. Baveno VIII: for GAVE requiring serial transfusions, band ligation is recommended over argon plasma coagulation — higher eradication rates and fewer sessions. Note that beta blockers and TIPS are PHG therapy, not GAVE therapy.

Baveno VIII 5.43Baveno VII 7.12

TIPS and rescue

The decision that most changes survival in this disease is who gets a shunt, and when. The consensus is explicit that it should be pre-emptive in high-risk patients rather than a salvage manoeuvre.

Pre-emptive — "early" TIPS

PTFE-covered stent within 72 hours, ideally under 24

For bleeding from oesophageal varices or GOV1 meeting any one of: Child-Pugh C, score 10–13; Child-Pugh B above 7 with active bleeding at the initial endoscopy; or HVPG ≥ 20 mmHg at the time of the bleed. Done after initial haemostasis, not instead of it. Improves one-year survival and bleeding control without a measurable increase in encephalopathy.

Baveno VIII 5.31

If you missed the 72-hour window

It is still worth doing

New in Baveno VIII, and it rescues a lot of real-world cases: TIPS placed within 2 weeks in Child-Pugh C 10–13, or within 1 week in Child-Pugh B above 7 with active bleeding, may still provide benefit. Late is better than never.

Baveno VIII 5.32

Do not talk yourself out of it

What is not an absolute contraindication

Baveno VIII is explicit: ACLF, overt hepatic encephalopathy, hyperbilirubinaemia, MELD score and severe alcohol-related hepatitis should not be considered absolute contraindications to pre-emptive TIPS. These are exactly the features that get sick patients excluded in practice.

Baveno VIII 5.33

Salvage TIPS

For failure — and do not screen people out of it

Failure to control variceal bleeding despite combined pharmacological and endoscopic therapy is best managed by salvage TIPS. Baveno VIII adds that it should be discussed for any refractory variceal bleeding regardless of age, Child-Pugh score and MELD score, case by case. The commonest error is leaving it too late, after repeated failed endoscopies — the second commonest is ruling the patient out on a score.

Baveno VIII 5.36–5.37

TIPS for recurrent or refractory ascites

≥ 3 large-volume paracenteses in a year

Should be considered irrespective of the presence of varices or a history of variceal bleeding. Baveno VIII adds that TIPS and transplant assessment should be discussed together, that the stent should be dilated to the smallest diameter that achieves an adequate response to limit overshunting, with stepwise dilation if ascites does not improve, and that renal function should be systematically assessed and CKD staged beforehand.

Refractory hepatic hydrothorax

TIPS should be considered

New explicit statement in Baveno VIII.

Baveno VIII 6.7

Age 70 and over

No longer a bar — case by case

The old age cut-off of 70 came from trial exclusion criteria rather than evidence of harm. Baveno VIII allows TIPS in older adults on a case-by-case basis.

Baveno VIII 6.8

Covered self-expanding metal stent

Preferred over balloon tamponade

A dedicated covered SEMS is preferred over balloon tamponade as bridge therapy to TIPS or transplantation. Provides tamponade for up to 14 days, with about 96% haemostasis and fewer complications. Baveno VIII states no maximum duration.

Baveno VIII 5.35

Balloon tamponade

Only if no stent is available — maximum 24 hours

Now explicitly second choice. Controls bleeding in around 90% but causes serious complications in about 20%, including aspiration and oesophageal perforation. Requires a protected airway. A bridge to TIPS or a stent, never a destination.

Baveno VIII 5.35

When TIPS is a poor idea

Relative and absolute limits

Heart failure, severe pulmonary hypertension, uncontrolled encephalopathy, active uncontrolled sepsis, biliary obstruction, extensive polycystic liver disease. EASL 2018 listed numeric bars — bilirubin above 3 mg/dL, platelets below 75, encephalopathy grade 2 or worse, age over 70, creatinine above 3 mg/dL — and AASLD flags MELD 18 or above as a poor candidate. Neither Baveno VIII nor AASLD endorses the EASL numeric list, and Baveno VIII explicitly softens the age bar.

Encephalopathy around TIPS

Rifaximin, and prior HE is not a bar

A history of overt HE is not an absolute contraindication to elective TIPS. Rifaximin should be considered for prophylaxis in TIPS candidates. If HE is recurrent or persistent after TIPS despite removing precipitants and optimal medical therapy, shunt embolisation and reduction or occlusion of the TIPS can be considered.

The recurring failure is treating pre-emptive TIPS as something to consider if things go badly. By then it is rescue TIPS, in a sicker patient, with worse outcomes. Identify Child-Pugh C 10–13 and Child-Pugh B with active bleeding at the time of the index endoscopy, and refer within 72 hours.

Decompensation

Baveno VIII devotes two whole panels to preventing and managing decompensation beyond the varices. This is where most of the change from Baveno VII sits.

What counts

Counts as decompensation

Clinically evident ascites (or hepatic hydrothorax) caused by portal hypertension, variceal bleeding, and/or overt hepatic encephalopathy, West Haven grade II or worse.

Baveno VIII 3.3

Does not count as decompensation

Minimal ascites, or ascites detectable only on imaging; covert hepatic encephalopathy; occult bleeding from portal hypertensive gastroenteropathy. Baveno VII said the data were insufficient to decide — Baveno VIII settles it as a definite exclusion, while noting that minimal ascites may still be associated with reduced survival.

Baveno VIII 3.4Baveno VII 5.6

Can precipitate decompensation or ACLF

Superimposed acute liver injury — alcohol-related hepatitis, acute hepatitis E, A or B, an HBV flare, autoimmune hepatitis, or drug-induced liver injury. Look for a precipitant rather than assuming natural progression, and look especially hard when jaundice is the only manifestation.

Ascites — the ladder

Grade 1 — only on ultrasound

No diuretics

Treat the cause. Baveno VIII is explicit that imaging-only ascites is not decompensation.

Grade 2 — moderate symmetrical distension

Sodium restriction + diuretics

Moderate sodium restriction plus a diuretic. For a first episode an aldosterone antagonist alone is often enough; long-standing ascites responds better to the combination.

Grade 3 — marked distension

Large-volume paracentesis first, then diuretics

Drain, replace albumin, then establish diuretics and sodium restriction.

Refractory

TIPS, transplant assessment, or palliative drainage

See the refractory block below. Every patient with decompensated cirrhosis should be assessed for transplant.

Vascular liver disease

Portal vein thrombosis, porto-sinusoidal vascular disorder and Budd-Chiari are the part of the consensus that most often gets missed. The single most important structural point: the rules for PVT in cirrhosis and PVT without cirrhosis are different, and mixing them up is the commonest error — most starkly over the thrombophilia workup, which is mandatory in one and explicitly discouraged in the other.

Measure the open lumen, not the clot. What it is

Obstruction to hepatic venous outflow anywhere from the small hepatic veins to the entry of the inferior vena cava into the right atrium. Secondary when the mechanism is extrinsic compression or endoluminal neoplastic or parasitic obstruction; otherwise primary. Presentation is extremely diverse, so consider it in any patient with acute, acute-on-chronic or chronic liver disease.

Where medical management alone is insufficient to improve symptoms and percutaneous recanalisation is not an option or has failed, TIPS is the recommended next step.

Stop all teratogenic medication including cytoreductive agents before conception. Stop vitamin K antagonists and DOACs as soon as the pregnancy is detected, and replace oral anticoagulants with therapeutic-dose LMWH throughout pregnancy.

Baveno VIII 8.16

Screen for varices in the second trimester

Unless adequate screening was done within the year before conception. Primary and secondary prophylaxis follow the same principles as outside pregnancy, including the use of propranolol.

Baveno VIII 8.17–8.18

Delivery

Absent obstetric contraindications, vaginal delivery is generally preferred over caesarean section in portal hypertension, provided the platelet count is above 20 × 10⁹/L.

Baveno VIII 8.19

Breastfeeding

NSBBs, heparin and warfarin are safe. Other vitamin K antagonists and all DOACs are contraindicated.

Baveno VIII 8.20

Chronic PVT in children

Also new. The paediatric pathway is not a scaled-down adult one — it is built around a surgical option that does not exist in adult practice.

Definition and workup

Chronic PVT in children is complete obstruction to normal portal venous flow to the liver — congenital or acquired, typically diagnosed on imaging. Work-up includes standard evaluation for chronic liver disorders; liver biopsy may be considered to rule out intrinsic liver disease, and investigation for thrombophilia is recommended.

Baveno VIII 9.29–9.30

Meso-Rex bypass is the centrepiece

Where there are features of portal hypertension, Meso-Rex bypass should be considered for pre-primary prophylaxis — that is, before varices even appear — as well as for primary prophylaxis of variceal haemorrhage and other portal hypertensive complications. A success rate above 90% requires established criteria, including patency of the intrahepatic portal venous system and surgical expertise.

Baveno VIII 9.31

Other primary prophylaxis is not established

The role of pharmacological, endoscopic and interventional radiological primary prophylaxis is not well established in children. Management of varices may need individualising for children without ready access to medical care.

Baveno VIII 9.32–9.33

Secondary prophylaxis

Meso-Rex bypass is the preferred approach. If it is not feasible: portal vein recanalisation, endoscopic treatment with or without an NSBB, or distal splenorenal shunting. Distal splenorenal shunting should be considered for recurrent variceal haemorrhage in the absence of hepatopulmonary syndrome, portopulmonary hypertension or encephalopathy.

Baveno VIII 9.34–9.35

Watch the shunts for life

With portal hypertension and/or portosystemic shunts — spontaneous or created — annual surveillance is warranted, especially for portopulmonary hypertension. Transplantation for potentially life-threatening, otherwise untreatable portal hypertensive complications.

Baveno VIII 9.36–9.37

Neonatal and cirrhotic PVT in children

Recent PVT is described in neonates with umbilical catheters. Spontaneous resolution can occur, so the role of anticoagulation is uncertain — monitor for clot extension, and consider anticoagulation where the thrombus is extensive. PVT with cirrhosis is uncommon in children, and the evidence for translating adult recommendations is insufficient.

Baveno VIII 9.38–9.39

After the bleed

Secondary prophylaxis — both, not either

NSBB plus band ligation, with carvedilol preferred over conventional NSBBs. The combination is first-line for preventing recurrent bleeding from oesophageal varices and is superior to either alone. Prescribing one and omitting the other is the commonest gap in follow-up. Baveno VIII 4.8

The banding programme

Repeat sessions every 1–4 weeks until varices are eradicated. Then endoscopy at 3–6 months, and every 6–12 months thereafter. Baveno VIII states no intervals for post-bleed banding or follow-up endoscopy — these are the established practice figures. practice, not Baveno

If combination therapy fails

Recurrent bleeding despite an adequate beta blocker and a completed banding programme is an indication for TIPS, and a trigger to consider transplant assessment.

Treat the cause — this outranks everything else

Alcohol abstinence, HBV suppression, HCV cure, weight loss and metabolic control in MASLD. Removing the aetiology lowers portal pressure, can reverse cACLD, and is the only intervention that changes the trajectory rather than managing its consequences.

The rest of the cirrhosis bundle

Six-monthly ultrasound for HCC. Nutrition and sarcopenia assessment. Hepatitis A and B, pneumococcal and influenza vaccination. Avoid NSAIDs and aminoglycosides. Review every drug for hepatic dosing — and review the PPI for withdrawal.

What Baveno VIII changed

The CSPH grey zone is now a calculation, not a table

The Baveno VII combination arms — 20–25 kPa with platelets under 150, 15–20 kPa with platelets under 110 — are replaced by an estimated probability of CSPH of 75% or more on ANTICIPATE, ANTICIPATE-NASH or NICER. And where the tests land in the indeterminate range, the answer is to re-evaluate at 12 months rather than force a decision.

Baveno VIII 1.16aBaveno VIII 1.17

Spleen stiffness gets harder numbers

Above 55 kPa on the 100 Hz probe rules CSPH in on its own. Below 40 kPa spares a screening endoscopy without needing the LSM and platelet pair at all.

Baveno VIII 1.16cBaveno VIII 1.19

Screening endoscopy intervals replace annual retesting

No varices at the index endoscopy means repeat at 2 years if the aetiology is still active, 3 years if it has been removed or suppressed. And patients already on an NSBB are stated outright not to need screening endoscopy at all.

Baveno VIII 1.20Baveno VIII 3.18

The transfusion target narrows to 7–8 g/dL

From the old 7–9 window, with explicit room to individualise for cardiovascular status, age and haemodynamic stability.

Baveno VIII 5.4

Vasoactive drugs for 2–5 days — or 24 hours

A 24-hour course may suffice after successful endoscopic haemostasis, provided a beta blocker is started afterwards. This is a real departure from running the infusion for five days by reflex.

Baveno VIII 5.7–5.8

Bleeding gastric varices: glue is now the bridge, TIPS the destination

For GOV2 and IGV1, cyanoacrylate or EUS-guided therapy achieves immediate haemostasis, but the preferred definitive treatment is TIPS with or without variceal embolisation, as soon as possible.

Baveno VIII 5.40–5.41

Missing the 72-hour TIPS window is no longer fatal to the plan

TIPS within 2 weeks in Child-Pugh C 10–13, or within 1 week in Child-Pugh B above 7 with active bleeding, may still provide benefit.

Baveno VIII 5.32

GAVE finally gets a treatment recommendation

Baveno VII only said GAVE must be distinguished from PHG. Baveno VIII: for GAVE needing serial transfusions, band ligation is recommended over argon plasma coagulation — better eradication, fewer sessions.

Baveno VIII 5.43

Salvage TIPS regardless of age, Child-Pugh and MELD

Discussed case by case for any refractory variceal bleeding. Together with the same softening for pre-emptive TIPS, the direction of travel is clear: stop using scores to rule people out of a shunt.

Baveno VIII 5.37

Covered SEMS is preferred over balloon tamponade

Stated outright as the bridge to TIPS or transplant, rather than the two being presented as alternatives.

Baveno VIII 5.35

Anticoagulation must not wait for variceal prophylaxis

Baveno VII said to band the high-risk varices first. Baveno VIII: start anticoagulation without delay, do not postpone it pending endoscopic prophylaxis. This settles a standing disagreement with AASLD in AASLD's favour.

Baveno VIII 8.10

DOACs move to first choice in vascular liver disease

From "LMWH then a vitamin K antagonist, DOAC in selected cases" to DOACs may be the primary oral choice — barring double or triple positive antiphospholipid syndrome, pregnancy, Child-Pugh C equivalent, or GFR under 30.

Baveno VIII 8.6

Occlusion is graded by remnant lumen, not clot burden

The VALDIG criteria: minimal if the remnant lumen is 50% or more, partial if under 50%, complete if zero — measured perpendicular to the main portal vein on portal-venous-phase imaging.

Baveno VIII 9.3

Bland versus tumour thrombus finally has criteria

Baveno VII offered none. Baveno VIII names A-VENA and LI-RADS — AFP above 1,000 ng/mL, venous expansion, thrombus neovascularity, thrombus adjacent to the tumour.

Baveno VIII 9.17

PVT screening extends beyond transplant candidates

Now recommended in patients with cirrhosis alongside HCC surveillance, not only in those being worked up for transplant.

Baveno VIII 9.16

Stop anticoagulation after TIPS

Routine anticoagulation following TIPS in cirrhosis with PVT is not recommended — and TIPS may be first-line where anticoagulation is contraindicated.

Baveno VIII 9.27–9.28

Non-cirrhotic PVT: a 3-month exit and a low-dose maintenance

Stopping at 3 months may be considered after complete recanalisation with a resolved reversible risk factor and no permanent one. Where there is no permanent major risk factor, long-term low-dose DOAC may be considered instead of full-dose — the evidence is rivaroxaban 15 mg daily.

Baveno VIII 9.8Baveno VIII 9.10

Budd-Chiari: stop letting the score decide

The BCS-TIPS Prognostic Index above 7 no longer triggers transplant over TIPS. Prognostic scores should not determine individual management alone. Biopsy is also no longer needed when imaging confirms the diagnosis, and a full HCC surveillance block appears for the first time — 6-monthly imaging and AFP, suspicion above 15 ng/mL, LI-RADS not applicable.

Baveno VIII 8.26Baveno VIII 8.28–8.30Baveno VIII 8.36

PSVD / NCPF rewritten end to end

Both names accepted under a 2026 multi-society agreement. Biopsy requirement drops from over 20 mm to 15 mm with one fragment of 10 mm. Diagnosis moves to a scoring system with major and minor histological criteria — NRH, muscularised portal venules and portal venule stenosis in 50% or more of tracts are the majors. Endoscopy can be spared where spleen stiffness is under 40 kPa and bilirubin is under 1 mg/dL.

Baveno VIII 8.39Baveno VIII 8.41–8.47

Two entirely new populations

Contraception and pregnancy in vascular liver disease, and chronic PVT in children — the latter built around Meso-Rex bypass, including for pre-primary prophylaxis before varices appear.

Baveno VIII 8.15–8.20Baveno VIII 9.29–9.39

A rule for what counts as a real change on repeat testing

Entirely new. A confirmed 30% relative change in liver stiffness marks a changed risk of liver-related events in both MASLD and alcohol-related cACLD — most meaningfully when it crosses 15 kPa. MR elastography uses 20%, ELF uses 0.5 units absolute, and pSWE/2D-SWE are not yet recommended for monitoring. And a 10% weight loss is what typically produces the 30% stiffness fall.

GLP-1s and SGLT2 inhibitors are explicitly safe in cACLD

For their approved indications. Meanwhile there is no approved drug for MASLD with established cirrhosis, and trial data from non-cirrhotic MASLD must not be extrapolated to it.

Baveno VIII 2.17Baveno VIII 2.19

Bariatric surgery gets a lower BMI threshold

MASLD-related cACLD without CSPH may itself count as an obesity-associated morbidity justifying surgery at a lower BMI than usual. With CSPH, complications rise and it belongs in an expert centre; in decompensated disease it is generally contraindicated.

Baveno VIII 2.14–2.16

Alcohol: abstinence, not moderation

Complete abstinence should be favoured over reduced consumption to lower liver-related events — agreed at 100%.

Baveno VIII 2.2

Recompensation is now measurable

12 months → more than 6 consecutive months, and "stable improvement in liver function" → a hard Child-Pugh A5/A6 target. Absence of recurrent variceal bleeding now counts even while the patient stays on carvedilol.

Baveno VIII 7.4

Further decompensation is defined by the index event

Baveno VII gave one undifferentiated list. Baveno VIII asks what decompensated the patient first, and defines further decompensation accordingly.

Baveno VIII 4.1

Minimal ascites, covert HE and occult PHG bleeding are definitively not decompensation

Baveno VII said the data were insufficient to decide.

Baveno VIII 3.4

Long-term primary antibiotic prophylaxis is no longer recommended

A reversal of Baveno VII 4.6, and a live disagreement with AASLD 2021.

Baveno VIII 4.12

Stop SBP prophylaxis on recompensation

New, and it closes a question EASL left explicitly open.

Baveno VIII 6.22

Statins are no longer recommended to prevent further decompensation

A step back from Baveno VII, which encouraged them. Their cardiovascular indication is untouched.

Baveno VIII 4.11

Terlipressin should preferably run as a continuous infusion

Starting at 2–3 mg/24 h, to reduce adverse events. The US label remains bolus-only.

Baveno VIII 6.19

New options in refractory ascites

Long-term albumin upgraded to "may be considered" in non-TIPS candidates; tunnelled peritoneal catheters and low-flow ascites pumps admitted as an alternative to repeated paracentesis.

Baveno VIII 6.10–6.11

TIPS opens up

Age 70 and over is case-by-case rather than excluded. Smallest effective stent diameter with stepwise dilation. Renal function and CKD staging are now a required part of the workup. ACLF, overt HE, hyperbilirubinaemia and MELD are not absolute contraindications to pre-emptive TIPS.

Antibiotic duration in acute variceal bleeding is individualised

No longer a fixed course of up to 7 days; Child-Pugh A patients may need a shorter course or none.

Baveno VIII 5.10

Withdraw the PPI

Unless there is a clear indication. And search exhaustively for a precipitant when jaundice is the only manifestation of further decompensation.
