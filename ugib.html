Who can go home

The first decision is not what to do, but whether the patient needs to be in hospital at all. One score answers it.

Glasgow-Blatchford ≤ 1

Discharge with outpatient endoscopy

The only score endorsed for the discharge decision, and all three bodies agree on the same threshold. ESGE: "Patients with a GBS ≤ 1 can be safely managed as outpatients with outpatient endoscopy" — strong recommendation, moderate evidence. ACG frames it by performance rather than a bare number: a score with a ≤ 1% false-negative rate for hospital-based intervention or death, giving GBS 0–1 as the worked example. Sensitivity at ≤1 is 98.6%, specificity 34.6%.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, moderateACG 2021 · Rec 1 · conditional, very low

Rockall

Predicts mortality — not a discharge tool

Pre-endoscopic Rockall uses age (<60 = 0, 60–79 = 1, ≥80 = 2), shock (pulse >100 with SBP >100 = 1; SBP <100 = 2) and comorbidity (cardiac, renal or hepatic disease = 2–3). The full score adds diagnosis and stigmata of recent haemorrhage after endoscopy. No guideline reviewed endorses it for the outpatient-discharge decision.

AIMS65

Predicts in-hospital mortality — explicitly not for discharge

One point each: Albumin <3.0 g/dL, INR >1.5, Mental status altered, Systolic BP ≤90, age >65. The international consensus explicitly discourages using it for discharge, because it "was designed to identify patients at high risk of death, not those at low risk." ESGE 2026 mentions no score other than GBS.

Timing of endoscopy

Within 24 hours

After haemodynamic resuscitation — universal agreement

Every guideline reviewed says the same thing: resuscitate first, then scope within 24 hours of presentation.

ESGE 2026 · StrongACG 2021 · Rec 5 · conditional, very low

The 12-hour rule is dead

Retracted by ACG, rejected by ESGE

"ESGE does not recommend emergent (≤6 hours) or urgent (≤12 hours) upper GI endoscopy unless the patient remains hemodynamically unstable despite adequate resuscitation." And ACG explicitly says it "did not include the suggestion from the 2012 ACG Guidelines that endoscopy within 12 hours may be considered in patients with high-risk clinical features." The only remaining trigger for a sub-24-hour scope is persistent instability despite adequate resuscitation.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, moderateACG 2021 · Rec 5

Why it changed

Lau, NEJM 2020

516 patients, all with GBS ≥ 12 — deliberately the high-risk group the 12-hour rule was aimed at. Endoscopy within 6 hours versus 6–24 hours. 30-day mortality 8.9% vs 6.6%, hazard ratio 1.35 (95% CI 0.72–2.54). Further bleeding 10.9% vs 7.8%. Urgent endoscopy found and treated more lesions — 60.1% vs 48.4% received endoscopic treatment — without improving any outcome. Numerically it was worse.

Lau NEJM 2020

Before the scope

Most of what determines the outcome happens here, in the first few hours — not at the endoscopy.

Transfusion — restrictive, with a cardiac carve-out

Hb < 7, or ≤ 8 with cardiovascular disease

No cardiovascular disease: transfuse below 7 g/dL, post-transfusion target 7–9 g/dL. With acute or chronic cardiovascular disease: transfuse at or below 8 g/dL, target ≥ 10 g/dL. ACG states a single 7 g/dL threshold with no cardiovascular exception in the recommendation itself — ESGE is the more clinically complete standard here.

ESGE 2026 · StrongACG 2021 · Rec 2 · conditional, lowguidelines differ

Erythromycin before the scope

250 mg IV, 30–120 minutes before endoscopy

For clinically severe or ongoing active bleeding. Clears the stomach, improves visualisation and reduces transfusion requirement. ESGE grades this strong with high-quality evidence; ACG grades the same drug at the same dose conditional with very-low-quality evidence — a striking confidence mismatch. ACG's window is 20–90 minutes. New in ESGE 2026: IV metoclopramide if erythromycin is unavailable, though no dose is given.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, highACG 2021 · Rec 3 · conditional, very low

Pre-endoscopic PPI

Permitted, unproven — and it must never delay endoscopy

ACG could not reach a recommendation for or against it. ESGE suggests it "be considered… however, this should not delay early endoscopy". Note the direction of travel: ESGE dropped its old "downstage the stigmata" rationale in 2026 and downgraded its certainty from high to low. The two bodies are converging on scepticism. Giving it is defensible; delaying the scope for it is not.

ESGE 2026 · Conditional, lowACG 2021 · Rec 4 · no recommendationguidelines differ

Tranexamic acid — do not give it

Strong recommendation against, high-quality evidence

"ESGE does not recommend the use of tranexamic acid in patients with acute nonvariceal UGIH." HALT-IT randomised 12,009 patients: death from bleeding at 5 days 3.7% vs 3.8% — no difference — with a significant increase in venous thromboembolism. This is one of the few strong, high-certainty negative recommendations in the whole field.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, HIGHHALT-IT 2020

Nasogastric lavage

Not recommended

"ESGE does not recommend the routine use of nasogastric or orogastric aspiration/lavage." It does not change management and it distresses the patient.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, moderate

Intubation

Only three indications

Routine prophylactic intubation is recommended against, at high certainty. Intubate only for ongoing active haematemesis, agitation, or encephalopathy with inability to control the airway. Routine intubation causes pneumonia.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, high / Strong, low

Video capsule and blood-sensing capsules

Not recommended

New negative recommendation in 2026 — no routine role in suspected upper GI bleeding.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, very low

Somatostatin and octreotide

Not for non-variceal bleeding

Their place is variceal bleeding. ESGE recommends against them here.

The adherent clot

The clearest ACG-versus-ESGE disagreement in the guideline set.

Both agree on the first step

Vigorous irrigation. ACG's statement applies only to clots that survive it. Many clots come off and reveal the real lesion underneath.

ESGE: remove it and treat what is beneath

"Patients with peptic ulcers presenting with an adherent clot (FIIb) should undergo endoscopic therapy, with clot removal and subsequent endoscopic hemostasis if indicated, provided that the endoscopist has the technical competence to safely remove the clot and manage potential conversion to a higher risk bleeding lesion." Note that ESGE lowered its certainty from moderate in 2021 to very low in 2026 and added the competence proviso — it is moving toward ACG's uncertainty.

ACG: cannot advise either way

"We could not reach a recommendation for or against endoscopic therapy in patients with UGIB due to ulcers with adherent clot resistant to vigorous irrigation."

Either way, the PPI is the same

ESGE gives full high-dose PPI to FIIb ulcers even when they are not treated endoscopically. If you leave the clot, the patient still gets the 72-hour regimen.

Achieving haemostasis

Epinephrine is never a monotherapy

The strongest agreement in the field

"ESGE does not recommend that epinephrine injection be used as endoscopic monotherapy. If used, it should be combined with a second endoscopic hemostasis modality." ACG agrees at strong strength. For actively bleeding ulcers the mandate is epinephrine plus contact thermal or mechanical therapy.

Dilution and volume: 1:10,000 or 1:20,000 in normal saline, injected in 0.5–2 mL aliquots in and around the ulcer base. Note that this figure survives only in the older ESGE 2015 text — neither ESGE 2026 nor ACG 2021 restates it, and no source specifies a total volume.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, HIGHACG 2021 · Rec 10 · strong

Thermal and mechanical modalities

The strongly-backed options

ACG recommends bipolar electrocoagulation, heater probe, or absolute ethanol injection at strong strength. It suggests clips, argon plasma coagulation, or soft monopolar electrocoagulation at conditional strength.

ACG 2021 · Rec 8 strong / Rec 9 conditional

A visible vessel can have monotherapy

The dual-therapy rule is about epinephrine, not about everything

For a non-bleeding visible vessel (FIIa), ESGE permits contact or non-contact thermal therapy, mechanical therapy, or a sclerosant — each as monotherapy or combined with epinephrine. Combination is mandated for actively bleeding FIa and FIb.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, moderate

Haemostatic forceps

Soft coagulation as monotherapy

May be used as monotherapy for high-risk stigmata — FIa, FIb and FIIa.

ESGE 2026 · Conditional, very low

Over-the-scope clips

Undisputed for rebleeding; contested as first-line

For recurrent bleeding both bodies endorse it, and the evidence is striking — further bleeding 15.2% with OTSC vs 57.6% with standard therapy in the Schmidt trial. For primary therapy the two now diverge: ESGE 2026 newly suggests OTS clips as first-line monotherapy for FIa, FIb and FIIa, on the basis of five RCTs showing lower 30-day rebleeding (RR 0.43). ACG restricts them to recurrent bleeding after previous successful haemostasis. ESGE's own certainty is very low.

ESGE 2026 · Conditional, very lowACG 2021 · Rec 12 · conditional, lowguidelines differ

Haemostatic powder (TC-325)

A bridge, not a treatment — and the societies contradict each other

ACG suggests it for actively bleeding ulcers. ESGE 2026 recommends against it as first-line monotherapy and restricts it to rescue when conventional modalities fail; the international consensus agrees with ESGE.

The limitation that settles it: TC-325 achieves over 90% immediate haemostasis, but the powder washes off within 12–24 hours. It does not seal a vessel. A patient treated with powder alone has not been definitively haemostased — it buys time to arrange repeat endoscopy, embolisation or surgery.

Not endorsed by anyone

ESGE 2021 recommended against routine use; ESGE 2026 softened to "could not reach a consensus for or against". ACG does not address it. Softening toward neutrality is not endorsement.

Rebleeding

Routine second-look endoscopy

Not recommended

"ESGE does not recommend routine second-look endoscopy as part of the management of nonvariceal UGIH" — strong, high certainty. ACG issues no recommendation but reveals its position methodologically: it excluded second-look studies from its meta-analyses because routine repeat endoscopy is "not a recommended or standard practice in the United States". Repeat endoscopy is driven by clinical evidence of rebleeding, not by protocol.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, HIGH

Definitions matter here

Persistent versus recurrent

Persistent bleeding is ongoing active bleeding refractory to standard haemostasis. Recurrent bleeding is bleeding after initially successful haemostasis. The escalation ladder is the same but the starting rung differs.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, high

First move for rebleeding: scope again

Not surgery, not interventional radiology

ACG: "repeat endoscopy and endoscopic therapy rather than undergo surgery or transcatheter arterial embolization." ESGE agrees at strong strength, and adds that an over-the-scope clip should be considered for this second attempt.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, highACG 2021 · Rec 15 · conditional

Then embolisation, then surgery

TAE is now ahead of surgery, not parallel to it

"Should this second attempt at endoscopic hemostasis also be unsuccessful, TAE should be considered. Surgery is indicated when TAE is either locally unavailable or after unsuccessful TAE." Surgery has become the fallback for embolisation failure or unavailability.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, moderateACG 2021 · Rec 16 · conditional, very low

Prophylactic embolisation

New concept in ESGE 2026

TAE after successful endoscopic haemostasis, in selected anatomically high-risk cases: haemodynamic instability at presentation, posterior duodenal wall location, ulcer larger than 2 cm, or where durable haemostasis is considered uncertain. Posterior duodenal means the gastroduodenal artery. Absent from ACG 2021.

ESGE 2026 · Conditional, very low
The ladder: repeat endoscopy, considering an over-the-scope clip → transcatheter arterial embolisation → surgery, only where embolisation is unavailable or has failed.

Antithrombotics

Scope warning. The ACG 2021 ulcer-bleeding guideline does not cover antithrombotics — its statements stop at haemostasis. The ACG position lives in a separate document, the ACG-CAG guideline on anticoagulants and antiplatelets during acute GI bleeding (2022). Citing "ACG 2021" for anticoagulation advice is a category error. That document and ESGE disagree on nearly every reversal agent.

Aspirin for secondary prevention — do not stop it

ESGE: "aspirin not be interrupted", strong recommendation. ACG-CAG: "suggest against holding the aspirin." If it is interrupted, restart on the day haemostasis is endoscopically confirmed (ACG-CAG) or within 3–5 days (ESGE).

ACG-CAG 2022 · conditional, very lowESGE 2026 · Strong, moderate

Why — the mortality signal

Sung's randomised trial in peptic ulcer bleeding on low-dose aspirin: continuing aspirin gave 30-day rebleeding 10.3% vs 5.4% (not statistically significant), but 30-day mortality 1.3% versus 9.0% in favour of continuing. Stopping aspirin roughly halves rebleeding and multiplies mortality several-fold. Patients die of myocardial infarction and stroke, not of their ulcer. This asymmetry is the single most commonly made error in this area.

Sung, Ann Intern Med 2010

Dual antiplatelet therapy after stenting

Continue the aspirin; interrupt only the second agent. Restart the P2Y12 inhibitor as soon as possible, preferably within 5 days. ESGE suggests cardiology consultation. ACG-CAG was unable to reach a recommendation on P2Y12 resumption.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, low

Warfarin

Withhold it. ESGE, if haemodynamically unstable: low-dose vitamin K plus IV prothrombin complex concentrate, or fresh frozen plasma if PCC is unavailable — and this must not delay endoscopy. ACG-CAG contradicts every element: it suggests against vitamin K, against FFP, and prefers PCC over FFP where something is given. Restart as soon as clinically indicated by thromboembolic risk.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, lowACG-CAG 2022 · conditional, very lowguidelines differ

Direct oral anticoagulants

Withhold, and do not delay endoscopy. ESGE: in severe ongoing bleeding, consider a DOAC reversal agent or IV PCC. ACG-CAG suggests against idarucizumab, against andexanet alfa, and against PCC for DOACs — all conditional, very low certainty. The two are reconcilable in practice: reversal is not routine, but remains an option in life-threatening haemorrhage.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, lowACG-CAG 2022 · conditional, very lowguidelines differ

Platelet transfusion

Not recommended routinely in patients on antiplatelet agents. Both bodies agree — a rare point of concord in this section.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, lowACG-CAG 2022 · conditional, very low

What INR is acceptable

Expert consensus: moderate coagulopathy, INR 1.5–2.5, should not delay endoscopy; correct above 2.5. ESGE restates the principle for both warfarin and DOAC patients — correction "should not delay endoscopy or if required, endoscopic hemostasis." Correct concurrently, never sequentially. Note these specific numbers come from secondary reporting of the international consensus, not from primary guideline text.

How to reconcile the conflict at the bedside. ACG-CAG's recommendations against reversal agents are all conditional with very-low certainty — they reflect absent GI-bleed-specific evidence and concern about thrombotic harm and cost, not evidence of ineffectiveness. Discharge and beyond

Test for H. pylori at the index endoscopy

And retest if it is negative

ESGE gives three strong, high-certainty recommendations: test in the acute setting at first endoscopy, treat if positive, retest those with a negative test at first endoscopy, and document successful eradication.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, HIGH

Why a negative test during bleeding means nothing

Sensitivity collapses in the presence of blood

In peptic ulcer bleeding specifically: rapid urease test sensitivity 0.73–0.86, urea breath test 0.73–0.89, stool antigen 0.72–0.80. Blood, PPI and antibiotics all suppress the organism, so index testing "underestimates the true prevalence". Every negative index test must be repeated after recovery, off PPI for at least 2 weeks. Missing this is a leading cause of preventable recurrent ulcer bleeding.

ESGE 2026meta-analysis, 40 studies

Who needs long-term PPI — and who does not

Four groups need it

1. Continuing an NSAID — use a COX-2 selective agent at the lowest effective dose plus a daily PPI, both, not either. 2. Continuing aspirin or another antithrombotic for secondary cardiovascular prevention. 3. On ongoing dual antiplatelet therapy — PPI co-therapy, strong recommendation. 4. Idiopathic ulcer, neither H. pylori nor NSAID related.

Who does not: an H. pylori-positive ulcer, successfully eradicated and documented, on no NSAID and no antithrombotic. Stop the PPI in this group — it is the commonest source of inappropriate indefinite PPI.

ESGE 2026 · Strong, moderate

Iron before discharge

New in ESGE 2026

Start iron therapy before the patient leaves in those with iron deficiency or anaemia. No dose, route or duration is specified.

ESGE 2026 · Conditional, low

Feed them early

Within 24 hours

Early oral nutrition within 24 hours of endoscopic haemostasis, where durable haemostasis has been achieved.

Non-ulcer lesions

ESGE 2026 and ACG 2021 both cover peptic ulcers only. ESGE explicitly hands off non-ulcer lesions to the CAG 2025 guideline on endoscopic management of non-variceal non-peptic-ulcer upper GI bleeding, which it endorses. All CAG recommendations below are conditional with very low certainty.

Dieulafoy lesion

Band ligation or through-the-scope clip, with or without epinephrine; contact thermocoagulation is an alternative. Epinephrine injection alone is explicitly recommended against. Cap-mounted clips cannot be ranked against conventional therapy for lack of data. Embolisation or surgery if endoscopy fails.

CAG 2025 · Recs 8–9

Mallory-Weiss tear

Treat only if it is actively bleeding. Band ligation or a clip is preferred over epinephrine injection alone. Recommended against treating non-bleeding visible vessels, adherent clots, clean bases or pigmented spots — the Forrest treat-or-not logic of peptic ulcers does not transfer. A non-bleeding lesion gets high-dose PPI alone.

CAG 2025 · Recs 5–7

Gastric antral vascular ectasia

Band ligation is now recommended over argon plasma coagulation — a reversal of long-standing practice, at conditional strength and very low certainty. Radiofrequency ablation is not recommended over APC.

CAG 2025 · Recs 10–11

Angioectasia and AVM

Endoscopic haemostasis is recommended but there is inadequate evidence to prefer any specific modality. Options in use: argon plasma coagulation, heater probe, bipolar and monopolar coagulation, band ligation.

ESGE 2015

Bleeding tumour

This is the one place haemostatic powder wins. CAG recommends topical haemostatic agents over conventional endoscopic therapy for malignant bleeding — the opposite of its rescue-only status in ulcers. A supporting trial reported 30-day rebleeding 2.1% vs 21.3% and immediate haemostasis 100% vs 68.6%. The diffuse, friable, oozing surface of a tumour suits a powder and defeats clips and thermal probes. But no endoscopic treatment has long-term efficacy — this is a bridge to oncological or radiotherapeutic control.

CAG 2025 · Recs 1–4

The disagreements worth knowing

Over-the-scope clips. ESGE 2026 newly permits them as first-line monotherapy for high-risk stigmata; ACG restricts them to recurrent bleeding. Strong agreement for rebleeding; first-line use is ESGE-only and at very low certainty. guidelines differ
Haemostatic powder. ACG suggests TC-325 for actively bleeding ulcers; ESGE and the international consensus restrict it to rescue after conventional modalities fail. The 12–24 hour wash-off supports the rescue-only reading. guidelines differ
Adherent clot. ESGE says remove it and treat what is underneath; ACG cannot recommend either way. Both irrigate first. guidelines differ
Reversal agents. ACG-CAG recommends against idarucizumab, andexanet alfa and PCC for DOAC-associated bleeding; ESGE says consider them in severe ongoing bleeding. guidelines differ
Vitamin K. ESGE gives low-dose vitamin K with PCC in the unstable warfarin patient; ACG-CAG suggests against vitamin K entirely. guidelines differ
Transfusion. ACG states a single 7 g/dL threshold; ESGE carves out 8 g/dL with a target above 10 for cardiovascular disease. guidelines differ
Erythromycin. Same drug, same dose, opposite confidence — ESGE strong with high certainty, ACG conditional with very low certainty. guidelines differ

Where they agree, emphatically

No tranexamic acid · no routine second-look endoscopy · no epinephrine monotherapy · no routine platelet transfusion · endoscopy within 24 hours and not within 12 · do not stop the aspirin · embolisation before surgery.
