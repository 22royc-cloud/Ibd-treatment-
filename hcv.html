The simplified algorithm

For treatment-naive adults, HCV treatment is now two decisions: is the patient eligible for the simplified pathway, and do they have cirrhosis. Everything else follows.

First — is the simplified pathway off the table?
  • Prior HCV treatment of any kindGo to the Retreat section — the regimen and duration are different
  • Decompensated cirrhosis — Child-Pugh ≥ 7, or any prior decompensationProtease inhibitors are contraindicated. Specialist management
  • HBsAg positiveHBV reactivation risk — boxed warning on every DAA. Needs concurrent HBV management
  • PregnancyDAAs are not established as safe in pregnancy; defer or refer
  • Known or suspected hepatocellular carcinomaSpecialist management
  • Prior liver transplantationSpecialist management — immunosuppressant interactions
  • End-stage renal disease on the cirrhosis pathwayRegimens still work without dose adjustment, but this sits outside the simplified algorithm
If none of those apply, the patient is on the simplified pathway — regardless of genotype, viral load or degree of fibrosis short of cirrhosis.

Then — cirrhosis or not

No cirrhosis · FIB-4 ≤ 3.25 and no other evidence

8 or 12 weeks

Glecaprevir/pibrentasvir — 8 weeks, three tablets daily with food
or
Sofosbuvir/velpatasvir — 12 weeks, one tablet daily

Compensated cirrhosis · Child-Pugh A, CTP < 7

8 or 12 weeks — the same regimens

Glecaprevir/pibrentasvir — 8 weeks, all genotypes
or
Sofosbuvir/velpatasvir — 12 weeks; genotype 3 needs baseline NS5A resistance testing first

How cirrhosis is decided here: presumed present if FIB-4 > 3.25, or if elastography, non-invasive serology, imaging, clinical findings or a previous biopsy say so. FIB-4 uses only age, AST, ALT and platelets — there is no excuse for not calculating it.

Pre-treatment workup

Within 6 months of starting — everyone

  • CBC
  • Hepatic function panel — AST, ALT, bilirubin, albumin
  • eGFR
  • Quantitative HCV RNA
  • HIV testing
  • HBsAg, anti-HBc and anti-HBs
  • Pregnancy test where relevant
  • FIB-4 calculation

Additionally, if cirrhosis is present

  • INR and Child-Pugh score
  • Liver ultrasound within 6 months
  • Genotype if using sofosbuvir/velpatasvir — genotype 3 needs baseline NS5A resistance testing
  • Endoscopy for varices per standard practice

Always

  • Full medication reconciliation including over-the-counter, herbal and recreational drugs
  • Formal drug–drug interaction check
  • Alcohol assessment
  • Reinfection risk and harm-reduction discussion

Retreatment after DAA failure

After almost any first failure

Sofosbuvir/velpatasvir/voxilaprevir for 12 weeks, all genotypes, with or without compensated cirrhosis. Add weight-based ribavirin for genotype 3 with cirrhosis.

Alternative for genotypes 1, 2, 4, 5, 6

Glecaprevir/pibrentasvir for 16 weeks — but not for genotype 3 with prior sofosbuvir or NS5A exposure.

Multiple failures, or failure of sofosbuvir/velpatasvir/voxilaprevir

Specialist referral. Options include sofosbuvir/velpatasvir/voxilaprevir with ribavirin for 24 weeks, or combining classes. Resistance testing has a role here, unlike in first retreatment.

Resistance testing is not routine before first retreatment. Three drug classes with distinct mechanisms overcome the substitutions that matter, and trials have not shown baseline NS5A resistance to reduce success.

Boxed warning on every DAA: hepatitis B reactivation, in some cases fulminant and fatal. Test HBsAg, anti-HBc and anti-HBs before treatment in every patient, without exception.
Check interactions formally, every time. The University of Liverpool HEP Drug Interactions database is the reference the guidelines point to. The recurring offenders are amiodarone, anticonvulsants, rifampicin, St John's wort, statins, proton pump inhibitors and antiretrovirals — but patients rarely volunteer herbal products unless asked directly.

Special populations

Decompensated cirrhosis — Child-Pugh B or C

No protease inhibitors. That rules out glecaprevir/pibrentasvir, sofosbuvir/velpatasvir/voxilaprevir and elbasvir/grazoprevir.

Use sofosbuvir/velpatasvir plus weight-based ribavirin for 12 weeks, or sofosbuvir/velpatasvir alone for 24 weeks if ribavirin cannot be tolerated. Manage in a centre with transplant access. With MELD above 20 or severe portal hypertension, transplantation may serve the patient better than antiviral therapy first — treating can improve liver function enough to lose transplant priority without restoring enough function to live well, the so-called MELD purgatory.

HBV coinfection

Boxed warning on every DAA. HBV reactivation during or after HCV treatment has caused fulminant hepatitis, hepatic failure and death.

Test HBsAg, anti-HBc and anti-HBs in everyone before starting. HBsAg-positive patients need HBV antiviral prophylaxis or treatment alongside. Anti-HBc-positive but HBsAg-negative patients need ALT and HBV DNA monitoring during and after therapy. This is the single most important safety check before writing the prescription.

HIV coinfection

Cure rates match HIV-negative patients. The difficulty is entirely drug interactions.

Check the antiretroviral regimen formally. Glecaprevir/pibrentasvir is contraindicated with atazanavir and not recommended with efavirenz or ritonavir-boosted darunavir or lopinavir. Tenofovir DF exposure rises with some regimens — monitor renal function. Do not stop or alter antiretrovirals to accommodate the DAA without the HIV team.

Chronic kidney disease, including dialysis

Both first-line pangenotypic regimens work without dose adjustment at every stage.

Glecaprevir/pibrentasvir achieved 97–98% SVR12 in CKD stage 3b–5. Sofosbuvir/velpatasvir reached about 95% in haemodialysis patients and has been FDA-approved below eGFR 30 since 2019. In kidney transplant candidates, timing relative to transplant is a transplant-centre decision — an HCV-viraemic donor organ may be an option.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

DAAs are not established as safe in pregnancy. Screen in every pregnancy.

Defer treatment until after delivery in most cases, and ribavirin is absolutely contraindicated. Vertical transmission is around 5%; it is not an indication for caesarean section, and breastfeeding is not contraindicated unless nipples are cracked or bleeding. Test the infant — HCV RNA from 2 months of age, or antibody after 18 months.

People who inject drugs

Active injecting is not a reason to withhold treatment. Cure rates are comparable.

Treat, and pair it with harm reduction: syringe services, opioid agonist therapy, and naloxone. Reinfection happens and is not a treatment failure — retreat. Annual HCV RNA testing while risk continues.

Acute HCV infection

Spontaneous clearance occurs in roughly 20–30%, usually within 6 months.

Current guidance favours treating without waiting, using the same regimens and durations, rather than the older practice of a 6-month observation period. Early treatment prevents onward transmission, which matters most in the networks where acute infection is being diagnosed.

Monitoring

The striking feature of modern HCV care is how little monitoring it needs. Resist the urge to add tests that no longer change anything.

Before treatment
WhatBloods within 6 months (3 months if cirrhotic), FIB-4, HBV and HIV serology, pregnancy test, medication review and interaction check
During treatment — no cirrhosis
WhatNo routine laboratory monitoring is required. That is a deliberate feature of the simplified algorithm, not an omission. Exceptions: check glucose in diabetes, since insulin requirements can fall, and monitor INR if on warfarin.
During treatment — compensated cirrhosis
WhatWatch clinically for decompensation. Any jaundice, ascites, encephalopathy or rising bilirubin, AST or ALT means stopping and seeking specialist input.
On ribavirin
WhatHaemoglobin at weeks 2 and 4, then monthly.
12 weeks or more after finishing
WhatQuantitative HCV RNA and a hepatic panel. Undetectable RNA at 12 weeks is SVR12 — cure.
If SVR achieved, F0–F2
WhatNo further liver follow-up needed. Discharge, with reinfection counselling.
If SVR achieved, advanced fibrosis or cirrhosis
WhatUltrasound every 6 months indefinitely, with or without AFP. Cure lowers HCC risk but does not remove it. Continue variceal surveillance per standard practice.
Ongoing exposure risk
WhatHCV RNA annually — antibody testing is useless after treatment, since it stays positive. Reinfection means retreatment, not blame.
SVR12 is cure. Late relapse after a documented SVR12 is very rare; a positive RNA afterwards in someone with ongoing exposure is far more likely reinfection than relapse. Distinguishing them changes what you do next — retreat either way, but address the exposure too.
The commonest post-cure error is discharging a patient with cirrhosis. Cure reduces HCC risk substantially but never to zero, and those patients need six-monthly ultrasound for life. The mirror error is keeping F0–F2 patients under surveillance they do not need.

About this page

Clinical decision support, not a prescribing authority. Adults, non-transplant setting. Verify against the current label and your local formulary. Decompensated cirrhosis, pregnancy, transplantation and multiple DAA failures all warrant specialist involvement rather than a protocol.
